# Readability score audit

**Decision.** Call the reported quantity *Brown word-order gain*, not a
readability score. It measures how much a text's observed word order outscores
deterministic shuffles under one Brown word-bigram model. It does not measure
whether people understand or enjoy the text.

## Question and evidence

The audit asks whether the scores currently associated with the manuscript can
be reproduced, what comparison they encode, and which claims those scores
support. The primary source is
[`readability-length-stratified-sentence-aware-20260925.json`](../runs/readability-length-stratified-sentence-aware-20260925.json),
produced by
[`score_length_stratified_readability.py`](../experiments/score_length_stratified_readability.py).
The selected candidate surfaces and their provenance are in
[`week_results.json`](week_results.json). The historical eleven-text table is
generated by [`build_readability_table.py`](build_readability_table.py).
The current manuscript uses the Diana pair in its appendix. Its main length
table comes from the separate v4 analysis in
[`READABILITY-LENGTH-CONSTRUCTION-RESULTS-20260926.md`](../experiments/READABILITY-LENGTH-CONSTRUCTION-RESULTS-20260926.md).

The frozen report covers eleven texts. Nine are selected longer constructions;
the other two are a 38-letter inherited example and a 54-letter construction.
Every text passes both the raw outside-in exactness scan and normalized
reversal equality. The origin and authorship of the 38-letter example are not
established by this audit.

## Metric and controls

The scorer trains an add-0.1 word-bigram model on 401 Brown documents and
reserves 99 disjoint Brown documents for controls. It lowercases ASCII words
matching `[A-Za-z]+`, so apostrophes split tokens. Candidate sentence
boundaries come from terminal punctuation; Brown controls retain Brown's
sentence boundaries. Unseen words map to `<unk>`, and each sentence receives
start/end transitions.

For each item, the report computes:

`Brown word-order gain = observed mean log probability - mean over 32 shuffles`

Each shuffle permutes the same word multiset and restores the original
sentence-length sequence. A positive score means the observed order has a
higher mean log probability than those shuffles in this model. Scores are in
nats per word transition. The shuffle seed is 20260925 and is keyed by item
identifier.

Each candidate is matched to a non-overlapping span of complete held-out Brown
sentences with the nearest scorer-token count. Hash tie-breaking makes span
selection deterministic and independent of score values. The report also
contains 108 non-overlapping Brown spans: 12 each at target lengths of 16, 32,
64, 128, 192, 256, 512, 1,024, and 2,048 scorer tokens. The model is not fit on
the control documents. Brown corpus and NLTK sources are cited in the
manuscript.

## Results that survive the audit

- Ten of the eleven matched Brown spans have higher order gain than their
  paired candidate. The 38-letter inherited example is the exception.
- For the nine longer constructions, all nine matched Brown spans score
  higher. Their mean paired gap is 1.631 nats per transition.
- All 108 length-stratified Brown spans score above their own shuffled
  baselines. Each of the nine target lengths has 12 positive controls.

The first two bullets describe comparisons under this fixed scorer. They do
not establish a population effect, a causal cost of palindrome constraints,
or a reader preference. Candidate selection is retrospective, most long
examples share construction history, and the Brown spans are corpus controls.
The last bullet is a control check for local word order, not readability
validation.

## Excluded exploratory report

The repository also contains an earlier 2026-09-25 score report from
[`score_week_results_readability.py`](../experiments/score_week_results_readability.py).
Its saved output reproduces exactly on rerun, aside from its timestamp. That
version trained on all 57,340 Brown sentences and selected its prose controls
from the same full corpus. Because the controls were not held out from model
training, its stronger score separation is in-sample. The manuscript uses the
document-held-out report above; it does not use the earlier result.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, install the recorded Python packages and Brown
corpus, then rerun the saved report check and regenerate the historical table:

```sh
python3 -m pip install -r paper/readability-requirements.txt
python3 -m nltk.downloader brown
python3 experiments/score_length_stratified_readability.py \
  --expected-report runs/readability-length-stratified-sentence-aware-20260925.json \
  --output /tmp/readability-length-stratified-rerun.json
python3 paper/build_readability_table.py
```

The run was repeated with Python 3.11.14, NLTK 3.10.3, wordfreq 3.1.1, and
the installed Brown corpus. The expected-report comparison passed for corpus
and split fingerprints, model settings, every candidate score, all 11 matched
controls, and all 108 length controls. It printed a non-fatal SciPy/NumPy
version warning from the environment; the scoring path uses the bundled NLTK
data with Python counters and logarithms. The source and expected report are
also included in the generated anonymous evidence archive.

## Readiness implication

The score audit is repeatable, but it cannot support a claim that these texts
are readable. A paper making a human-language-quality claim still needs a
prespecified blind reader study with intact-prose and shuffled controls,
separate ratings for grammaticality and whole-text coherence, and an
agreement report. No responses exist in the current evidence.
