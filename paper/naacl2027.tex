\begin{abstract}
Exact palindrome generation combines a global character constraint with lexical and sentence-level demands. We describe two search implementations and examine 66 saved output records containing 52 distinct strings. Their collection used project-specific length bands and resource settings; it supplies no established performance baseline. Thirty-six records contain the phrase \emph{one poem association}. Against 32 permutations of each output's own words, Brown and GPT-2 assign higher mean log probability to the original order for 40/66 and 37/66 records. These comparisons measure sensitivity to word order under fixed scorers. DeepSeek V3.2 ties all 66 candidate--shuffle pairs. Qwen3 32B assigns all 52 distinct candidates zero for grammaticality and coherent meaning; 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 shuffled controls pass its joint rubric. Qwen2.5-3B fails the same control screen. The recorded outputs thus have positive likelihood contrasts in some cases and zero absolute rubric scores throughout. No human ratings or matched evaluation of a published generator establish improved readability or generation performance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome has the same sequence of letters when read backward after spaces, punctuation, and case are removed. Word boundaries need not reverse, so a list of words can satisfy exactness without forming grammatical sentences or expressing coherent meaning. Character equality is mechanically checkable; language quality requires separate evaluation.

\citet{norvig2002palindrome} describes residual-character search for long palindromes; \citet{papadopoulos2015palindromes} use n-gram graphs and caution that length alone says little. \citet{bonlarron-regin-2024} combine linguistic constraints with language-model perplexity to select outputs.

We study 66 saved output records from two project implementations. The central question is what their measured properties establish: exactness, output distinctness, likelihood relative to shuffled controls, and absolute model ratings. We define the comparison underlying each language diagnostic and report its scope. The cited methods provide context; this study contains no reproduction of a published generator under matched conditions and makes no claim of generation improvement.

\section{Construction and evaluation}

\paragraph{Exact search.}
Let $T(x)$ retain only lowercase ASCII letters from a rendered text $x$. Exactness requires $T(x)=\mathrm{reverse}(T(x))$. Both search implementations start from empty text and use a shared frozen vocabulary and lexical gate. Outside-in search extends the outer word sequences toward their meeting point, tracking the unmatched letters owed by one side to the other; it closes when that residual is itself a palindrome. Center-out search extends away from the center and closes only on empty overhang. These native closure rules are retained, so the comparison concerns the complete implementations.

The vocabulary comes from a frozen 30K word list filtered through an English lexicon. Words of at most two letters must belong to a fixed 32-form allowlist. The shared basic gate checks supported ASCII rendering, exact palindrome equality, the target length band, lexicon membership, the short-word rule, and exclusions for listed catalogue items and templates. Repeated words are allowed. An eligible closure is rendered as space-joined words with an initial capital and final period.

Search uses English Zipf frequencies from \code{wordfreq} 3.1.1 \citep{speer-2022-wordfreq}. Its heuristic adds 0.3 per word character and subtracts 2 for each prior use of the word and 4 for an adjacent repetition. A seeded pseudorandom generator adds a fresh uniform perturbation in $[0,0.4)$ to each child's accumulated score for beam ranking. This perturbation is excluded from the score used to select the best eligible closure per normalized letter. Both implementations cap partial texts at 32 words, residual overhang at 24 letters, and text length at the target band's ceiling. Brown, GPT-2, and the prompted judges are applied afterward and do not select outputs.

\paragraph{Output collection.}
The archive contains 240 runs: 20 seeds (4000--4019), three target bands (30--49, 50--79, and 80--119 normalized letters), two implementations, and two resource configurations. Seeds perturb search trajectories on one vocabulary; they are not independent linguistic tasks. The bands and limits were development choices, not a published benchmark. One configuration uses beam/candidate/per-parent limits of 60/200/8 and a 45-second cooperative budget; the other uses 256/800/32 and 180 seconds. Both have a 400-step ceiling. These settings followed an earlier configuration with no eligible outputs.

Only the second configuration returns texts: 66 selected records from its 120 runs. All 240 outcomes remain in the archive. Four resource settings change together, and the search implementations have different closure rules. These records establish how the diagnostic sample was collected; they do not establish a method advantage, a resource-efficiency gain, or an effect of output length. We do not treat the unsuccessful configuration as an established generation baseline.

\paragraph{Defined word-order controls.}
Let $s_M(x)$ be the mean log probability assigned to text $x$ by scorer $M$. For $K=32$ saved or reproducibly seeded word permutations $\pi_k$, the diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
D_M(x)=s_M(x)-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_M(\pi_k(x)).
\end{equation}
Each permutation retains the item's word multiset and per-sentence word counts. Brown pools and repartitions words; GPT-2 reassigns words to fixed surface slots, retaining punctuation and whitespace. GPT-2 subword-token counts can change. Thus $D_M(x)>0$ means that the candidate's order receives a higher score than the mean of its own shuffled controls. The controls are word-order perturbations, not outputs of a competing generator or a human readability standard.

For Brown, $s_M$ is the mean add-0.1 word-bigram log probability in nats per word transition, trained on 401 Brown documents with 99 held out \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}. Scoring lowercases alphabetic tokens, maps unseen tokens to \code{<unk>}, and inserts sentence boundaries. Shuffles are generated by an item-keyed deterministic seed.

For GPT-2, $s_M$ is mean causal log probability per predicted token, using \url{openai-community/gpt2} at revision \url{607a30d783dfa663caf39e06633721c8d4cfcd7e}. It scores 32 archived permutations per record. Brown and GPT-2 use different permutation realizations and different scoring units; their contrast magnitudes are not directly comparable. Both are applied after search and do not select the sample.

\paragraph{Prompted judgments and controls.}
DeepSeek V3.2 receives one counterbalanced candidate--shuffle pair per output with condition labels hidden (Bedrock, temperature 0, top-p 1, maximum 256 output tokens) \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekv32}. Its choices are candidate, shuffle, or tie.

Separate Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and hosted Qwen3 32B audits score grammaticality and coherent meaning on 0--3 scales \citep{qwen25,awsbedrockqwen332b}. The grammar anchors are: 0, no recoverable sentence parse; 1, isolated grammatical phrases with major sentence breaks; 2, a mostly recoverable but strained parse; 3, natural grammatical connected English. The meaning anchors are: 0, no recoverable proposition or event; 1, word associations with unclear relations; 2, a plausible but incomplete or vague interpretation; 3, clear meaning with understandable relations. The prompt instructs judges to assess the exact text without repair or rewards for formal symmetry.

Each audit has 244 presentations: 52 unique candidates, three shuffles per candidate, 12 held-out Brown prose spans and their shuffles, and 12 hidden repeats. Labels and item IDs are hidden. A joint pass requires at least 2 on both dimensions. The frozen control screen requires at least 8/12 prose controls and at most 3/12 prose shuffles to pass. These thresholds are study choices. The screen checks sensitivity to the selected prose and shuffled controls; it does not calibrate the rubric against human judgments or validate quality distinctions among palindromes. Both audits use temperature 0; settings and responses are archived. Hosted calls have no provider seed.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Mechanical validity and output variety.}
The 66 outputs contain 52 distinct strings, 26 per implementation. Every distinct string passes the normalized-letter reversal check; none reverses the word sequence. A further mechanical screen accepts 54/66 selected outputs (28 outside-in; 26 center-out). That screen requires distinct content words and excludes whole-word reversals, repeated multiword units, proper palindromic subspans, and listed templates. It is a post-selection check, rather than a rerun of search under stricter admission.

The phrase \emph{one poem association} occurs in 36/66 outputs. The first center-out output in the frozen seed order is:
\begin{quote}\small
In as if one poem association no it a i cos same open of is an i.
\end{quote}
Its 48-letter tape is exact. The example shows that letter equality and word membership leave word order and sentence meaning unconstrained. There are 66 outputs but 52 distinct strings, so both counts are needed to describe variety.

\paragraph{Likelihood relative to shuffled controls.}
Brown contrasts are positive for 40/66 output records, and GPT-2 contrasts for 37/66. These denominators count selected records, including repeated strings, rather than 66 independent texts. The two scorers need not agree, because their language models, tokenization, scoring units, and shuffle realizations differ. A positive contrast supplies no threshold for grammaticality or meaning. We do not interpret subgroup means over the project-selected length bands as a method ranking or an effect of length.

\paragraph{Relative preference and absolute ratings.}
DeepSeek ties all 66 candidate--shuffle pairs. In 38 responses its feature note is \emph{Both are word salad with no coherent meaning}. Table~\ref{tab:judges} reports the absolute-rating checks. Qwen2.5 assigns all candidates zero on both dimensions but passes only 1/12 prose controls; a separate post-hoc single-sentence sensitivity check passes 4/12. Both fail the control gate, making that judge's candidate scores inconclusive.

Under Qwen3, 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 prose shuffles pass jointly, satisfying the same screen. It still assigns all 52 candidates zero on both dimensions. None of their 156 matched shuffles passes jointly either, giving a candidate-minus-shuffle pass-rate difference of zero percentage points. Hidden repeats agree on both scores in 11/12 pairs, compared with 12/12 for Qwen2.5. Seven of the Qwen3 prose controls contain multiple sentences; all seven pass, as do 4/5 single-sentence controls. The control results show separation between these 12 prose controls and their shuffled versions under this rubric; the 0/52 candidate result leaves no distinctions among the palindromes.

\section{Discussion}

Brown and GPT-2 compare each candidate with shuffled versions of its own words; Qwen3 rates grammar and meaning and separates its prose controls from their shuffles. The 11/12 versus 0/12 control result shows separation under this rubric, while the 0/52 candidate result leaves no ranking among the palindromes. Neither result measures how people read the strings. Human ratings are needed to test whether these diagnostics track readers' grammaticality and meaning judgments \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}.

The search heuristic rewards word frequency and character count and penalizes local repetition; it has no sentence-grammar or meaning objective. None of the saved comparisons establishes a generation advance over a published system. Such a claim would require a specified comparator, matched vocabulary and inputs, common acceptance and selection rules, an explicit compute budget, and outcomes measured under that protocol. The present resource configurations and length bands define one output collection. Reproducible internal settings alone do not establish a meaningful benchmark.

\section*{Reproducibility and assistance}

The supplement \url{v1-reproducibility.zip} contains frozen manifests, all requested search outcomes, selected texts and shuffles, scorer source, model revisions, prompts, and saved responses. Hash-checked joins link every score to its generated text. The descriptive archive analysis and rubric totals can be regenerated offline; Brown and GPT-2 can be rescored with the pinned dependencies. Fresh hosted-model requests may differ. Brown corpus text is omitted under its terms. Generative AI assisted implementation, writing, and internal review. No participant data were collected.

\section{Limitations}

The study uses one vocabulary, three length bands, and 20 seed clusters; seeds vary search trajectories rather than independent semantic tasks. The adaptive resource follow-up changes four settings together and compares implementations with different closure rules, so it cannot attribute coverage to one setting or establish that one generator is superior. Language diagnostics condition on successful search. Of 66 outputs, 52 are distinct and 36 contain the phrase \emph{one poem association}. Brown and GPT-2 are fixed likelihood models; DeepSeek compares one candidate--shuffle pair per output; Qwen3 has 12 prose controls, seven of them multi-sentence. Qwen pretraining exposure to Brown is unknown, and Bedrock exposes neither a model-weight digest nor a provider seed. No human ratings validate the absolute rubric or establish readability. The retrospective construction example in Appendix~\ref{sec:diana} is separate from the 240-run study.

\section{An existing construction case: the Diana frame}
\label{sec:diana}

A separate sentence-aware Brown audit records an inherited 38-letter reference and a 54-letter extension obtained through bounded noun-phrase grammar intersection over its open character frame:
\begin{quote}\small
An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.

An aide rips nine memo-hero memos. Some more home men inspire Diana.
\end{quote}
Both texts are exact letter-level palindromes. The extension preserves the inherited frame while adding noun-phrase material; it has 13 scorer tokens and two sentences, compared with nine tokens and one sentence in the reference. The audit does not establish the reference's authorship.

Under the Brown diagnostic defined above, the respective contrasts with 32 own-word shuffles are 0.5152 and 0.4071 nats per transition. Two retrospective prose controls matched on scorer-token count have contrasts of 0.0251 and 1.9235; each contains one sentence. Sentence counts and lexical content therefore differ across these comparisons. Neither the reference sentence nor these prose controls establishes a baseline for a readability improvement. The saved pair documents a particular exact construction and its model scores. Neither text was included in the 52-candidate Qwen audits, and neither has project human ratings. Complete surfaces, score records, source hashes, and the offline analysis accompany the supplement.
