\begin{abstract}
A palindrome can satisfy exact character reversal while leaving sentence structure unresolved. We examine what automatic language diagnostics reveal about 66 saved output records containing 52 distinct texts. Brown bigram and GPT-2 shuffle-relative likelihood contrasts have opposite signs for 28/52 texts. Looking beyond the shuffle mean, 63/66 records score above some of their 32 word permutations and below others. The outputs also share lexical fragments: 46/52 distinct texts contain \emph{no it a}. Grouping by another recurring phrase gives different descriptive patterns under the two scorers. We visualize these continuous scores, permutation spreads, and phrase concentrations, with hash-checked offline reproduction. Prompted judges provide no ranking within the candidate set. This case study shows why exactness, output diversity, and sensitivity to word order need separate reporting; none alone establishes human readability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A letter-level palindrome reads identically backward after spaces, punctuation, and case are removed. Word boundaries need not reverse. Exactness is mechanically checkable, but the resulting word sequence can still lack grammatical sentences or coherent meaning.

Comparing an output's likelihood with permutations of its own words holds lexical content fixed and tests preference for its ordering. The contrast's sign alone discards its magnitude and the spread across permutations. It also hides whether many nominally distinct outputs reuse the same fragments. We therefore ask what becomes visible when a saved palindrome collection is examined through continuous contrasts, individual shuffle scores, and phrase prevalence. The resulting figures expose scorer disagreement and lexical concentration that a pass count cannot describe.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\citet{norvig2002palindrome} describes residual-character search, and \citet{papadopoulos2015palindromes} generate palindromes using n-gram graphs. For constrained RADNER sentences, \citet{bonlarron-regin-2024} combine constraint programming, linguistic properties, and perplexity-based curation. We do not reproduce or rank these generators. Our focus follows criterion-specific metric checks \citep{sai-etal-2021-perturbation} and explicit evaluation dimensions \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty}: we specify what each diagnostic measures and inspect its behavior on one exact-palindrome collection.

\section{Collection and diagnostics}
\paragraph{Search and sample.}
Let $T(x)$ retain lowercase ASCII letters from text $x$. Exactness requires $T(x)=\mathrm{reverse}(T(x))$. Two implementations start from empty text with a shared vocabulary filtered from a frozen 30K word list. Outside-in search extends outer word sequences toward their meeting point and closes when the unmatched residual is itself a palindrome. Center-out search grows from the center and closes on empty overhang. Their closure rules differ, so they do not isolate search direction alone.

The shared gate checks ASCII rendering, exactness, target length, lexicon membership, a fixed 32-form allowlist for words of at most two letters, and listed catalogue/template exclusions. Repetition is allowed. Search ranks states by word frequency, character count, repetition penalties, and seeded perturbations, selecting the eligible closure with highest unperturbed score per normalized letter. Language diagnostics are applied afterward. Appendix~\ref{sec:search-details} gives heuristic details.

The archive contains 240 runs: 20 seeds (4000--4019), three bands (30--49, 50--79, 80--119 normalized letters), two implementations, and two resource bundles. Beam/candidate/per-parent limits and cooperative time budgets are 60/200/8 with 45 seconds, or 256/800/32 with 180 seconds; both allow 400 steps. The latter bundle alone supplies 66 outputs, 33 per implementation, containing 52 distinct texts. The settings followed earlier null runs. These project choices provide sample provenance; four limits change together, seeds vary search trajectories, and language diagnostics condition on success. No generation advantage or length effect is claimed.

\paragraph{Likelihood contrasts.}
For scorer $M$, let $s_M(x)$ be mean log probability. With $K=32$ own-word permutations $\pi_k$, define
\begin{equation}
D_M(x)=s_M(x)-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_M(\pi_k(x)).
\end{equation}
Brown uses add-0.1 word-bigram log probability in nats per transition, trained on 401 Brown documents with 99 held out \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}. It lowercases alphabetic tokens, maps unseen tokens to \code{<unk>}, and inserts sentence boundaries. Item-keyed shuffles pool words and repartition them to the original sentence lengths.

GPT-2 uses mean causal log probability per predicted token \citep{radford2019language} at the pinned revision in \citet{huggingfacegpt2}. Its archived shuffles reassign words to fixed surface slots, preserving punctuation, whitespace, and per-sentence word counts; subword-token counts can change. Brown and GPT-2 therefore differ in model, units, tokenization, and permutation realizations. A positive contrast describes preference relative to the shuffle mean, without a readability or significance threshold.

\paragraph{Continuous and lexical analyses.}
For GPT-2 we also retain each individual difference $d_k(x)=s_M(x)-s_M(\pi_k(x))$, whose mean is $D_M(x)$. Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}b shows its minimum, maximum, and quartiles for every record. Repeated texts have separate item-keyed controls. For text-level plots, we average contrasts across the records with an identical surface hash; every copy agrees on both contrast signs before collapsing. Each of the 52 texts then has equal weight.

We count lowercase ASCII-word trigrams with at most one occurrence vote per distinct text, retaining the six most frequent (alphabetical tie-breaking). We separately group texts by the previously noted phrase \emph{one poem association}. Both analyses are retrospective descriptions; the overlapping trigrams and phrase groups are not independent treatments.

\paragraph{Supplementary model ratings.}
DeepSeek V3.2 compares one counterbalanced candidate--shuffle pair per record \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekv32}. Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3 32B rate grammaticality and coherent meaning from 0 to 3 \citep{qwen25,awsbedrockqwen332b}. A joint pass requires at least 2 on both. Their chosen control screen requires at least 8/12 held-out prose controls and at most 3/12 prose shuffles to pass. This checks sensitivity to those controls, without human calibration. Appendix~\ref{sec:judge-details} gives the rubric and full accounting.



\section{Results}
\paragraph{Scorer disagreement.}
All 52 distinct texts pass exact letter reversal; none reverses word order. Brown and GPT-2 contrasts are positive for 40/66 and 37/66 records, respectively. At text level, Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}a has 15 points positive under both scorers, nine nonpositive under both, and 28 with opposite signs. Its off-diagonal cases remain after duplicates are collapsed. For example, B has the largest GPT-2 contrast ($+0.598$ per predicted token) but a negative Brown contrast ($-0.312$ nats per transition). The figure describes disagreement; the differing controls prevent attributing it to the models alone.

\paragraph{Variation across permutations.}
For 63/66 records, the original GPT-2 score lies strictly inside the range of its 32 shuffle scores (Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}b). Two originals exceed all 32 shuffles and one scores below all 32. Thus a positive mean contrast usually coexists with some shuffles that score better than the original. These extrema concern a finite control set and are not significance tests or uncertainty intervals for human quality.

\paragraph{Fragment concentration.}
The trigram \emph{no it a} occurs in 46/52 distinct texts (88.5\%); \emph{a i cos} and \emph{it a i} each occur in 44/52. Text distinctness therefore overstates lexical variety if considered alone. Figure~\ref{fig:fragments} shows that \emph{one poem association} appears in 24/52 texts, corresponding to 36/66 records. Their mean Brown contrast is $+0.201$, versus $-0.003$ for the other 28 texts; their mean GPT-2 contrast is $-0.013$, versus $+0.110$. This contrast in descriptive group patterns motivates inspecting shared fragments, but cannot identify which words cause a score difference.

\paragraph{Rubric context.}
Qwen3 assigns all 52 candidates zero on both dimensions, with 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 prose shuffles passing jointly. Qwen2.5 fails its control screen, and DeepSeek ties every pair. These checks supply no quality ranking within the candidates; the continuous and lexical analyses describe variation the rubric floor leaves unresolved.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
The useful distinction is between a relative score and the properties of the resulting language. In this collection, two likelihood diagnostics disagree in sign for more than half the distinct texts, the individual shuffle scores usually straddle the original, and most distinct texts share a short fragment. Reporting only exactness, a positive shuffle mean, or a judge pass count would omit these patterns.

We recommend reporting continuous contrasts with their permutation spreads and inspecting recurrence across distinct outputs. This is a diagnostic workflow, not a validated readability measure. Establishing reader quality requires a separate human study with explicit dimensions and quality checks \citep{khashabi-etal-2022-genie}; a generation-advance claim also requires a matched comparator and compute budget.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The supplement contains all search outcomes, selected texts and shuffles, scorer sources, model revisions, prompts, and saved responses. The offline figure builder checks exact-text joins and reproduces both figures and their numerical data; Appendix~\ref{sec:figure-reproduction} gives commands. No new search or model inference was run. Brown corpus text is omitted under its terms.

\section*{Limitations}
This is one success-conditioned collection from one vocabulary and 20 search seeds, not a sample of independent semantic tasks. Resource settings and native closure rules differ. Duplicate collapse removes repeated surfaces but not shared fragments or search dependence. The plots and phrase groups are retrospective, without prespecified inferential tests. Shuffle ranges depend on the 32 sampled permutations; they are neither exhaustive nor confidence intervals. Brown and GPT-2 use different controls and units. The 12 Qwen prose controls have no human calibration; Qwen pretraining exposure to Brown is unknown. Hosted models expose no provider seed or weight digest. There are no human ratings or matched external-generator results. The Diana construction case in Appendix~\ref{sec:diana} is separate from this collection.

\section*{Ethical considerations}
No participant data were collected. Model ratings must not be presented as human judgments. Generative AI assisted implementation, writing, and internal review.

\section{Search heuristic and bounds}
\label{sec:search-details}
Search uses English Zipf frequencies from \code{wordfreq} 3.1.1 \citep{speer-2022-wordfreq}. Its heuristic adds 0.3 per word character and subtracts 2 for each prior use of the word and 4 for an adjacent repetition. A seeded pseudorandom generator adds a fresh uniform perturbation in $[0,0.4)$ to each child's accumulated score for beam ranking. This perturbation is excluded from the score used to select the best eligible closure per normalized letter. Both implementations cap partial texts at 32 words, residual overhang at 24 letters, and text length at the target band's ceiling. The heuristic has no sentence-grammar or meaning objective.

\section{An existing construction case: the Diana frame}
\label{sec:diana}

A separate sentence-aware Brown audit records an inherited 38-letter reference and a 54-letter extension obtained through bounded noun-phrase grammar intersection over its open character frame:
\begin{quote}\small
An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.

An aide rips nine memo-hero memos. Some more home men inspire Diana.
\end{quote}
Both texts are exact letter-level palindromes. The extension preserves the inherited frame while adding noun-phrase material; it has 13 scorer tokens and two sentences, compared with nine tokens and one sentence in the reference. The audit does not establish the reference's authorship.

Under the Brown diagnostic defined above, the respective contrasts with 32 own-word shuffles are 0.5152 and 0.4071 nats per transition. Two retrospective prose controls matched on scorer-token count have contrasts of 0.0251 and 1.9235; each contains one sentence. Sentence counts and lexical content therefore differ across these comparisons. Neither the reference sentence nor these prose controls establishes a baseline for a readability improvement. The saved pair documents a particular exact construction and its model scores. Neither text was included in the 52-candidate Qwen audits, and neither has project human ratings. Complete surfaces, score records, source hashes, and the offline analysis accompany the supplement.

\section{Supplementary judge protocol}
\label{sec:judge-details}
DeepSeek V3.2 receives one counterbalanced candidate--shuffle pair per record, with condition labels hidden (Bedrock, temperature 0, top-p 1, maximum 256 output tokens). Its choices are candidate, shuffle, or tie.

Each Qwen audit contains 244 presentations: 52 distinct candidates, three shuffles per candidate, 12 held-out Brown prose spans and their shuffles, and 12 hidden repeats. Labels and item IDs are hidden. Both audits use temperature 0. The prompt asks judges to assess the exact text without repair or rewards for formal symmetry.

The grammar anchors are: 0, no recoverable sentence parse; 1, isolated grammatical phrases with major sentence breaks; 2, a mostly recoverable but strained parse; 3, natural grammatical connected English. The meaning anchors are: 0, no recoverable proposition or event; 1, word associations with unclear relations; 2, a plausible but incomplete or vague interpretation; 3, clear meaning with understandable relations.

Qwen2.5 passes 1/12 prose controls and 0/12 shuffles; its separate post-hoc single-sentence check passes 4/12 and 0/12. Both fail the chosen screen, making its candidate ratings inconclusive. Under Qwen3, no candidate or its 156 matched shuffles passes jointly. Seven prose controls contain multiple sentences; all seven pass, as do 4/5 single-sentence controls. Hidden repeats agree on both scores in 11/12 Qwen3 pairs and 12/12 Qwen2.5 pairs.
