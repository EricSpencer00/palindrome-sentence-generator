\begin{abstract}
Exact palindrome generation separates a mechanically checkable character constraint from grammaticality and meaning. We examine automatic language diagnostics on 66 saved output records containing 52 distinct strings. Brown bigram and GPT-2 likelihoods favor the original word order over 32 own-word shuffles for 40/66 and 37/66 records, respectively. Both contrasts are positive for 15 distinct texts, yet Qwen3 32B assigns every candidate zero for grammaticality and coherent meaning. The same rubric passes 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 shuffled controls. DeepSeek V3.2 ties every candidate--shuffle pair; Qwen2.5-3B fails the prose-control screen. This case study documents positive shuffle-relative likelihood alongside floor-level absolute model ratings, including after duplicate outputs are collapsed. It supports separate reporting of exactness, relative likelihood, and rubric scores. Without human ratings, these diagnostics establish neither readability nor a validated quality ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome has the same sequence of letters when read backward after spaces, punctuation, and case are removed. Word boundaries need not reverse, so a list of words can satisfy exactness without forming grammatical sentences or expressing coherent meaning. Character equality is mechanically checkable; language quality requires separate evaluation.

One tempting diagnostic compares an output's language-model likelihood with permutations of its own words. This holds lexical content fixed, but asks a relative question: does the model prefer the original order? It does not ask whether the original is grammatical or conveys a recoverable meaning. Palindromes provide a useful setting for examining this distinction because a text can satisfy the character constraint while leaving both language properties unresolved.

We ask whether positive shuffle-relative likelihood co-occurs with positive absolute model ratings in one archived collection. Our contribution is a reproducible case analysis: we define the controls, join diagnostics to exact texts, and cross-tabulate likelihood signs at both record and distinct-text levels. Fifteen distinct texts receive positive contrasts from both likelihood scorers and zero scores from the absolute judge that passes our prose-control screen. This is an observation about the specified diagnostics and sample, not a human assessment of those texts.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\citet{norvig2002palindrome} describes residual-character palindrome search, and \citet{papadopoulos2015palindromes} generate palindromes using n-gram graphs. In a different constrained-generation task, \citet{bonlarron-regin-2024} combine constraint programming, linguistic properties, and perplexity-based curation for RADNER sentences. We do not reproduce these generators or compare performance with them. Our evaluation focus is closer to criterion-specific metric checks \citep{sai-etal-2021-perturbation} and explicit definitions of human-evaluation dimensions \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty}. Those works motivate specifying what a control tests; our study examines own-word shuffles and absolute model rubrics on exact palindromes, without human calibration.

\section{Construction and evaluation}

\paragraph{Exact search.}
Let $T(x)$ retain only lowercase ASCII letters from a rendered text $x$. Exactness requires $T(x)=\mathrm{reverse}(T(x))$. Both search implementations start from empty text and use a shared frozen vocabulary and lexical gate. Outside-in search extends the outer word sequences toward their meeting point, tracking the unmatched letters owed by one side to the other; it closes when that residual is itself a palindrome. Center-out search extends away from the center and closes only on empty overhang. These native closure rules are retained, so the comparison concerns the complete implementations.

The vocabulary comes from a frozen 30K word list filtered through an English lexicon. Words of at most two letters must belong to a fixed 32-form allowlist. The shared basic gate checks supported ASCII rendering, exact palindrome equality, the target length band, lexicon membership, the short-word rule, and exclusions for listed catalogue items and templates. Repeated words are allowed. An eligible closure is rendered as space-joined words with an initial capital and final period.

Search ranks states using word frequency, character count, repetition penalties, and seeded perturbations. Each run selects the eligible closure with the highest unperturbed heuristic score per normalized letter. Brown, GPT-2, and the prompted judges are applied afterward and do not select outputs. Appendix~\ref{sec:search-details} gives the heuristic and caps.

\paragraph{Output collection.}
The archive contains 240 runs: 20 seeds (4000--4019), three target bands (30--49, 50--79, and 80--119 normalized letters), two implementations, and two resource configurations. Seeds perturb search trajectories on one vocabulary; they are not independent linguistic tasks. The bands and limits were development choices, not a published benchmark. One configuration uses beam/candidate/per-parent limits of 60/200/8 and a 45-second cooperative budget; the other uses 256/800/32 and 180 seconds. Both have a 400-step ceiling. These settings followed an earlier configuration with no eligible outputs.

Only the second configuration returns texts: 66 selected records from its 120 runs, 33 per implementation. All 240 outcomes remain in the archive. Four resource settings change together, and the implementations have different closure rules. We use these runs as sample provenance, with no claim of a method advantage, resource-efficiency gain, or length effect.

\paragraph{Defined word-order controls.}
Let $s_M(x)$ be the mean log probability assigned to text $x$ by scorer $M$. For $K=32$ saved or reproducibly seeded word permutations $\pi_k$, the diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
D_M(x)=s_M(x)-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}s_M(\pi_k(x)).
\end{equation}
Each permutation retains the item's word multiset and per-sentence word counts. Brown pools and repartitions words; GPT-2 reassigns words to fixed surface slots, retaining punctuation and whitespace. GPT-2 subword-token counts can change. Thus $D_M(x)>0$ means that the candidate's order receives a higher score than the mean of its own shuffled controls. The controls are word-order perturbations, not outputs of a competing generator or a human readability standard.

For Brown, $s_M$ is the mean add-0.1 word-bigram log probability in nats per word transition, trained on 401 Brown documents with 99 held out \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}. Scoring lowercases alphabetic tokens, maps unseen tokens to \code{<unk>}, and inserts sentence boundaries. Shuffles are generated by an item-keyed deterministic seed.

For GPT-2, $s_M$ is mean causal log probability per predicted token \citep{radford2019language}, using the pinned model revision in \citet{huggingfacegpt2}. It scores 32 archived permutations per record. Brown and GPT-2 use different permutation realizations and scoring units; their contrast magnitudes are not directly comparable. A positive sign is a descriptive comparison with a finite shuffle mean, not a significance test.

\paragraph{Prompted judgments and controls.}
DeepSeek V3.2 receives one counterbalanced candidate--shuffle pair per output with condition labels hidden (Bedrock, temperature 0, top-p 1, maximum 256 output tokens) \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekv32}. Its choices are candidate, shuffle, or tie.

Separate Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and hosted Qwen3 32B audits score grammaticality and coherent meaning on 0--3 scales \citep{qwen25,awsbedrockqwen332b}. The grammar anchors are: 0, no recoverable sentence parse; 1, isolated grammatical phrases with major sentence breaks; 2, a mostly recoverable but strained parse; 3, natural grammatical connected English. The meaning anchors are: 0, no recoverable proposition or event; 1, word associations with unclear relations; 2, a plausible but incomplete or vague interpretation; 3, clear meaning with understandable relations. The prompt instructs judges to assess the exact text without repair or rewards for formal symmetry.

Each audit has 244 presentations: 52 unique candidates, three shuffles per candidate, 12 held-out Brown prose spans and their shuffles, and 12 hidden repeats. Labels and item IDs are hidden. A joint pass requires at least 2 on both dimensions. The frozen control screen requires at least 8/12 prose controls and at most 3/12 prose shuffles to pass. These thresholds are study choices. The screen checks sensitivity to the selected prose and shuffled controls; it does not calibrate the rubric against human judgments or validate quality distinctions among palindromes. Both audits use temperature 0; settings and responses are archived. Hosted calls have no provider seed.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Mechanical validity and output variety.}
The 66 outputs contain 52 distinct strings, 26 per implementation. Every distinct string passes the normalized-letter reversal check; none reverses the word sequence. A further mechanical screen accepts 54/66 selected outputs (28 outside-in; 26 center-out). That screen requires distinct content words and excludes whole-word reversals, repeated multiword units, proper palindromic subspans, and listed templates. It is a post-selection check, rather than a rerun of search under stricter admission.

The phrase \emph{one poem association} occurs in 36/66 outputs. The first center-out output in the frozen seed order is:
\begin{quote}\small
In as if one poem association no it a i cos same open of is an i.
\end{quote}
Its 48-letter tape is exact. It has a positive Brown contrast (0.6788 nats per transition) but a negative GPT-2 contrast ($-0.0712$ log probability per predicted token). Letter equality and lexicon membership alone impose no sentence-level grammar or meaning requirement.

\paragraph{Likelihood relative to shuffled controls.}
Brown contrasts are positive for 40/66 records, and GPT-2 contrasts for 37/66. Table~\ref{tab:overlap} gives their overlap. Both are positive for 21 records representing 15 distinct texts. At least one is positive for 56 records representing 43 distinct texts. Every one of these texts receives zero on both Qwen3 dimensions. The observation therefore persists when duplicate strings are counted once.

Repeated copies have their own item-keyed shuffle controls. We collapse by exact surface-text hash only after checking that every copy agrees on both contrast signs; all do. These are finite-sample counts, not independent trials or estimates over a population of palindromes. The scorers differ in model, tokenization, units, and shuffle realizations, so the cross-tab cannot isolate the reason for their disagreement.

The control results show separation between these 12 prose controls and their shuffled versions under this rubric; the 0/52 candidate result leaves no distinctions among the palindromes.

\section{Discussion and implications}

The likelihood and rubric results answer different questions. By construction, $D_M(x)>0$ is possible even when $x$ has low likelihood: its shuffles need only score lower. Table~\ref{tab:overlap} provides concrete cases where this relative preference coexists with floor-level absolute ratings. It does not establish that the likelihood models are wrong or that Qwen3 is a human-quality oracle. Qwen3 separates the selected prose controls from their shuffles but supplies no ranking among candidate palindromes.

For this task, we recommend reporting exactness and output distinctness alongside the full diagnostic definition, the absolute rubric distribution, and the judge's prose-control results. A failed control screen makes candidate ratings inconclusive; a passed screen establishes sensitivity only to those controls. A claim about readers would require a separately administered human study with explicit dimensions and quality checks \citep{khashabi-etal-2022-genie}. A generation-advance claim would additionally require a specified comparator with matched inputs, acceptance rules, output selection, and compute budget.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The supplement contains manifests, all search outcomes, selected texts and shuffles, scorer source, pinned model revisions, prompts, and saved responses. Hash-checked joins link scores to texts. Its offline audit, \path{paper/audit_diagnostic_overlap.py}, regenerates Table~\ref{tab:overlap} and checks its Qwen3 text joins without inference. Brown and GPT-2 can be rescored with pinned dependencies; fresh hosted-model requests may differ. Brown corpus text is omitted under its terms.

\section{Conclusion}
On this collection, exact palindrome equality and positive likelihood relative to own-word shuffles coexist with zero absolute model ratings. The overlap includes 15 distinct texts favored by both likelihood scorers. Keeping the three measurements separate makes that outcome visible without treating a relative likelihood gain as evidence of readability.

\section*{Limitations}

The study uses one vocabulary, three length bands, and 20 seed clusters; seeds vary search trajectories rather than independent semantic tasks. The adaptive resource follow-up changes four settings together and compares implementations with different closure rules, so it cannot attribute coverage to one setting or establish that one generator is superior. Language diagnostics condition on successful search. Of 66 outputs, 52 are distinct and 36 contain the phrase \emph{one poem association}. Collapsing duplicates does not remove shared phrases or search dependence. The likelihood cross-tab is a retrospective reanalysis, not a preregistered hypothesis test. Brown and GPT-2 use different shuffles; DeepSeek sees one shuffle per output. Qwen3 has only 12 prose controls, and its pretraining exposure to Brown is unknown. Bedrock exposes neither a model-weight digest nor a provider seed. No human ratings validate the absolute rubric or establish readability. The separate construction example in Appendix~\ref{sec:diana} lies outside the 240-run study.

\section*{Ethical considerations}
No participant data were collected. The ratings reported here are model outputs and must not be presented as human judgments. Generative AI assisted implementation, writing, and internal review.

\section{Search heuristic and bounds}
\label{sec:search-details}
Search uses English Zipf frequencies from \code{wordfreq} 3.1.1 \citep{speer-2022-wordfreq}. Its heuristic adds 0.3 per word character and subtracts 2 for each prior use of the word and 4 for an adjacent repetition. A seeded pseudorandom generator adds a fresh uniform perturbation in $[0,0.4)$ to each child's accumulated score for beam ranking. This perturbation is excluded from the score used to select the best eligible closure per normalized letter. Both implementations cap partial texts at 32 words, residual overhang at 24 letters, and text length at the target band's ceiling. The heuristic has no sentence-grammar or meaning objective.

\section{An existing construction case: the Diana frame}
\label{sec:diana}

A separate sentence-aware Brown audit records an inherited 38-letter reference and a 54-letter extension obtained through bounded noun-phrase grammar intersection over its open character frame:
\begin{quote}\small
An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.

An aide rips nine memo-hero memos. Some more home men inspire Diana.
\end{quote}
Both texts are exact letter-level palindromes. The extension preserves the inherited frame while adding noun-phrase material; it has 13 scorer tokens and two sentences, compared with nine tokens and one sentence in the reference. The audit does not establish the reference's authorship.

Under the Brown diagnostic defined above, the respective contrasts with 32 own-word shuffles are 0.5152 and 0.4071 nats per transition. Two retrospective prose controls matched on scorer-token count have contrasts of 0.0251 and 1.9235; each contains one sentence. Sentence counts and lexical content therefore differ across these comparisons. Neither the reference sentence nor these prose controls establishes a baseline for a readability improvement. The saved pair documents a particular exact construction and its model scores. Neither text was included in the 52-candidate Qwen audits, and neither has project human ratings. Complete surfaces, score records, source hashes, and the offline analysis accompany the supplement.
