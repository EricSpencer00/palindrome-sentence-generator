\begin{abstract}
Letter-level palindromes must match after reversing their letters, even when words and sentences do not. We examine a retrospectively selected sequence of four paired edits from 568 to 752 letters. At each step, an authored left proposal was matched to a chart-selected opposing resegmentation. A separate first-success search on a fixed grammar of 10 names, three predicates, and four-clause chains saved one 672-letter result. Two exhaustive implementations of that grammar found the same 38,498 accepted ordered chain pairings; the saved result occurs once in this set. The printed endpoint is exact but rough and repetitive. A Brown word-bigram score provides a local-order diagnostic; no human ratings were collected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome reads the same after its letters are reversed, ignoring case, spaces, and punctuation. The short example \emph{An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.} has 38 letters under this definition. It is an inherited reference, not a result produced by the constructions studied here. Extending an exact tape is straightforward if words do not matter; finding longer text that also reads naturally is harder because word boundaries and sentence structure must fit the character constraint.

Prior palindrome systems assemble corpus n-grams or extend unmatched character remainders \citep{papadopoulos2015palindromes,norvig2002palindrome}. Our paired edits modify an exact parent: an authored left proposal meets a chart-selected opposing resegmentation. A separate finite subject-predicate-object chart connects four-clause chains while matching opposing clauses character by character. We report the saved construction path, a comparison of two implementations of that bounded search, and exact validation. The complete 752-letter endpoint appears below. Constrained language generation provides broader context \citep{bonlarron-regin-2024}; a Brown word-bigram comparison with intact prose measures local order, not reader understanding.

\section{Paired edits}

For rendered text $x$, let $\norm{x}$ lowercase and keep only ASCII letters. An exact palindrome satisfies $\norm{x}=\rev(\norm{x})$. This property of the rendered string does not establish grammar or meaning. Work below is on normalized letter strings. Write an exact parent as
\begin{equation}
 p=AqC\rev(q)\rev(A), \qquad C=\rev(C),
\end{equation}
where $q$ is the unchanged bridge between the edit sites and $C$ is the center. Insert equal-length strings $U$ and $V$ on opposite sides of the center:
\begin{equation}
 p'=AUqCV\rev(q)\rev(A).
\end{equation}
The edited tape is exact if and only if
\begin{equation}
 Uq=q\rev(V). \label{eq:seam}
\end{equation}
After the mirrored outer string $A$ is set aside, this equality matches the two strings adjacent to $C$; reversing it matches the corresponding suffixes. Equation~\ref{eq:seam} describes the first insertion in the 568-to-640 step. The three later lineage edits replace paired spans, so we validate each complete output directly rather than applying the insertion result beyond its assumptions.

\section{Saved construction path}

The selected lineage grows from 568 to 752 letters in four edits: one paired insertion and three paired-span replacements. At each stage, an AI-assisted left event or span proposal was paired with a bounded chart search for the opposing resegmentation. Each stage inherits its parent and is not an independent trial. A deterministic first-success relation-chain search saved one exact 672-letter tape. Its fixed setup has 10 proper names, 3 predicates, four-clause chains, normalized seam cuts at 48 and 520, and a 160-key relation-count index. The archived replay reproduces that result. Its counter of 9,273 sums right-clause candidates considered before each character filter, not unique states or outputs. Without early stopping, an exhaustive comparison applied online character-residual matching and an offline reverse-pair index to the same grammar and gates: clause continuity, distinct and historically absent relations, no self-palindromic clauses, and predicate diversity. Both enumerated the same set of 38,498 accepted ordered pairs of four-clause chains (identical candidate-key digest); the saved 672-letter surface matches one row. Each implementation recorded 1,739,558 left-clause expansions and 1,321,024 matched counterpart rows. The online implementation recorded 162,580 character steps; the indexed implementation used 300 clause rows and 160 directed reverse-pair edges. These operator counts are limited to this finite search space. The anonymous archive includes both implementations and sanitized inputs for a full rerun. Across the named examples, exactness was checked by a raw-text outside-in scan that skips nonletters and by equality of the normalized tape and its reversal; recorded lengths and SHA-256 digests also match. The archive's lineage diffs replay saved tapes, not historical generation.

\section{A local-order diagnostic}

Nine retrospective outputs from 498 to 752 letters, including related lineage stages, are paired with contiguous Brown spans matched by token count. A 54-letter output and an inherited 38-letter reference are excluded. These descriptive outputs share construction history and are not an independent sample.

\emph{Order gain} is sentence-aware word-bigram log probability minus the mean for 32 same-token shuffles preserving sentence lengths. The model is trained on 401 Brown documents; controls come from a disjoint 99-document pool, with $\alpha=0.1$ smoothing and unseen words mapped to \code{<unk>}. Candidate sentence boundaries follow their punctuation; Brown controls retain corpus sentence boundaries. The 108 additional intact controls span nine lengths from 16 to about 2,050 tokens; matched and length-control spans are selected by token-count closeness and deterministic SHA tie-breaking, without overlap or score-based selection. The split, spans, and full scorer are in the evidence archive \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}.

In the saved 32-shuffle diagnostic, each matched prose span scores higher than its paired output (mean gains 1.522 vs. $-0.109$ nats per transition); all 108 additional spans score above their own shuffle means. Token matching controls length, not vocabulary, genre, or meaning. The statistic measures local order and sentence boundaries, not syntax, coherence, or reader preference.

\section{Discussion}

The paired-edit condition preserves exactness in this saved 568-to-752 lineage; a separate bounded construction reaches 672. The repository uses the 568-letter causal-scene tape as the parent for continued work, although longer exact descendants exist. The inherited 38-letter reference remains its only established reader-facing benchmark. The printed endpoint shows the remaining quality gap: length alone does not establish fluent English.

The Brown comparison reports one local-order statistic on a retrospectively selected, dependent set. It has no demonstrated relationship to readers' judgments and is not an evaluation of candidate readability. Human judgments of readability, coherence, and meaning remain necessary; standard evaluation work recommends explicit criteria and reproducible protocols for such judgments \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}. No reader study was conducted here.
