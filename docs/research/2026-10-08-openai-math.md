# OpenAI math release and the palindrome research decision

Checked October 8, 2026 against the primary sources linked below.

The newest math announcement found is [Sharing AI progress in mathematics](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/), dated October 6. It releases manuscripts, reasoning summaries, attempted-problem statistics, and some Lean proofs. OpenAI describes the model as internal; this announcement does not establish that an available public model can reproduce those results.

The [official repository](https://github.com/openai/math) currently lists 719 manuscripts in 372 related families. It reports approximately 4,000 posed problems and roughly three hours of Pro-equivalent thinking per result on average. The repository says approximately 42% of top-line results are formalized and explicitly warns that unformalized results may have issues. These are author-reported research claims, not 719 independently established discoveries or a 372/4,000 success rate: papers, families, and attempted problems are different units.

The [August 1 release](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) spans geometry, codes, groups, circuit complexity, quantum games, lattices, and combinatorics. None of the inspected announcements provides a ready-made theorem or algorithm that establishes readable palindrome generation. Treating their headline results as evidence for this project's generator would be unsupported.

## Decision applied here

Use the transferable research practices: independent certificates, explicit claim boundaries, failed-attempt denominators, provenance, and finite obstructions before larger searches. This is our methodological inference from the releases, not a claim made by OpenAI about palindromes.

1. Keep the paired 360-job fragment-penalty study as a within-generator intervention. Its matched baseline is meaningful, but it does not establish human readability.
2. Independently recount letters, opposing characters, hashes, and structural checks for every new output.
3. Reject the proposed central-shell clause grammar before search: all 120 actor/patient endpoint pairs are incompatible. Record that finite certificate instead of reporting a 500,000-state search that never needed to run.
4. Run a frozen word-cap feasibility probe on the NUC. The only experimental change is 32 versus 256 permitted words; exactness and strict admission are separate outcomes. Preserve all sixteen job outcomes and the interrupted first execution.
5. Keep public paper and package labels at v1. New constructions are exploratory until independently verified; readability still requires an administered reader study.
