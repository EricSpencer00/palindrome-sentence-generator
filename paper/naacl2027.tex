\begin{abstract}
Letter-level palindromes must match after reversing their letters, even as the text is divided into words and sentences. We document a paired-edit construction in one saved lineage from 568 to 752 letters, alongside a bounded search whose saved result is an exact 672-letter tape. In an exhaustive comparison on that search's fixed grammar, online residual matching and an offline reverse-pair index return the same 38,498 exact candidates. We verify each full tape independently and replay the saved character edits; this does not replay the original generation procedure. The printed endpoint is mechanically exact but rough and repetitive, and no human ratings were collected. A Brown word-bigram shuffle comparison is included only as a local-order diagnostic, not as evidence of readability. This case study records a construction path, not a comparative or general claim about fluent generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome reads the same after its letters are reversed, ignoring case, spaces, and punctuation. The short example \emph{An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.} has 38 letters under this definition. It is an inherited reference, not a result produced by the constructions studied here. Extending an exact tape is straightforward if words do not matter; finding longer text that also reads naturally is harder because word boundaries and sentence structure must fit the character constraint.

Prior palindrome systems have enumerated strings assembled from corpus n-grams or extended unmatched character remainders \citep{papadopoulos2015palindromes,norvig2002palindrome}. In our saved paired-edit lineage, an authored event proposal is matched to an opposing resegmentation under a fixed seam; unlike those approaches, the lineage edits an existing exact parent rather than constructing a palindrome from a corpus or remainder. The separate 672-letter search uses a chart over finite subject-predicate-object clauses, enforcing object-to-subject continuity across four-clause chains while filtering each opposing clause against the reverse character residual. That residual resets at clause boundaries. We compare two exhaustive implementations of this bounded search, not against the prior systems. This is a specific hybrid workflow, not a new symmetry principle or a demonstrated advantage over prior systems. Constraint-based language generation provides broader context for composing text under lexical and structural restrictions \citep{bonlarron-regin-2024}.

We report exactness, length, the saved construction path, and a separate 672-letter search result. The paper prints the 752-letter endpoint so readers can inspect what the construction produced. A word-bigram comparison with intact Brown-corpus spans is secondary context only: it tests local word-order preference against shuffles of the same words and cannot establish whether a reader finds a passage understandable.

\section{Paired edits}

For rendered text $x$, let $\norm{x}$ lowercase and keep only ASCII letters. An exact palindrome satisfies $\norm{x}=\rev(\norm{x})$. Work below is on normalized letter strings. Write an exact parent as
\begin{equation}
 p=AqC\rev(q)\rev(A), \qquad C=\rev(C),
\end{equation}
where $q$ is the unchanged bridge between the edit sites and $C$ is the center. Insert equal-length strings $U$ and $V$ on opposite sides of the center:
\begin{equation}
 p'=AUqCV\rev(q)\rev(A).
\end{equation}
The edited tape is exact if and only if
\begin{equation}
 Uq=q\rev(V). \label{eq:seam}
\end{equation}
After the mirrored outer string $A$ is set aside, this equality matches the two strings adjacent to $C$; reversing it matches the corresponding suffixes. Equation~\ref{eq:seam} describes the first insertion in the 568-to-640 step. The three later lineage edits replace paired spans, so we validate each complete output directly rather than applying the insertion result beyond its assumptions.

A separate bounded relation-chain search returned one exact 672-letter tape. Its fixed setup has 10 proper names, 3 predicates, four-clause chains, normalized seam cuts at 48 and 520, and a 160-key relation-count index. The archived deterministic replay reproduces the saved result. Its counter of 9,273 sums the live right-clause frontier sizes before each residual-character filter; it is not a count of unique search nodes or candidate chains. An exhaustive comparison applied online character-residual matching and an offline reverse-pair index to the same grammar and gates. Both enumerated the same 38,498 exact candidates (identical candidate-set digest); each recorded 1,739,558 left-clause expansions and 1,321,024 matched counterpart rows. The online implementation recorded 162,580 character steps; the indexed implementation used 300 clause rows and 160 reverse-compatible pairs. These are operator counts, not runtime or quality comparisons, and the result is limited to this finite search space. The anonymous archive includes both implementations and sanitized inputs for a full rerun. Across the named examples, exactness was checked by a raw-text outside-in scan that skips nonletters and by equality of the normalized tape and its reversal; recorded lengths and SHA-256 digests also match. The archive's lineage diffs replay saved tapes, not candidate generation.

\section{A local-order diagnostic}

Nine retrospective outputs from 498 to 752 letters, including related lineage stages, are paired with contiguous Brown spans matched by token count. A 54-letter output and an inherited 38-letter reference are excluded. These descriptive outputs share construction history and are not an independent sample.

\emph{Order gain} is sentence-aware word-bigram log probability minus the mean for 32 same-token shuffles preserving sentence lengths. The model is trained on 401 Brown documents and evaluated on 99 held-out documents, with $\alpha=0.1$ smoothing and unseen words mapped to \code{<unk>}. The 108 additional intact controls span nine lengths from 16 to about 2,050 tokens; matched and length-control spans are selected by token-count closeness and deterministic SHA tie-breaking, without overlap or score-based selection. The split, spans, and full scorer are in the evidence archive \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}.

\section{Discussion}

The paired-edit condition preserves exactness in this saved 568-to-752 lineage; a separate bounded construction reaches 672. These are selected construction artifacts, not readability incumbents: the repository retains the 568-letter causal-scene row as its working-length incumbent, while the inherited 38-letter reference remains its only established reader-facing benchmark. Neither construction establishes a general advantage or fluent English. The printed endpoint makes the remaining quality gap visible rather than treating length as a proxy for prose quality.

The Brown comparison reports one local-order statistic on a retrospectively selected, dependent set. It has no demonstrated relationship to readers' judgments and is not an evaluation of candidate readability. Human judgments of readability, coherence, and meaning remain necessary; standard evaluation work recommends explicit criteria and reproducible protocols for such judgments \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}. No reader study was conducted here.
