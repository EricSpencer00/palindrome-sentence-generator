\begin{abstract}
Exact palindrome generation couples a global character constraint with the lexical and sentence-level demands of language. We examine how construction success, output length, and automatic language diagnostics relate on the same generated texts. A paired study of two search implementations, three length bands, and two resource bundles yields 66 basic-valid outputs in 120 runs under the larger bundle, compared with none under the original bundle. These outputs contain 52 distinct strings; 36 contain the exact phrase \emph{one poem association}. Brown word-order gain and GPT-2 likelihood exceed same-word shuffle baselines for 40/66 and 37/66 outputs, respectively. Yet DeepSeek V3.2 ties all 66 candidate--shuffle pairs, and a Qwen3 32B rubric assigns all 52 distinct candidates zero grammaticality and coherent-meaning scores with joint passes for 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 shuffled controls. A smaller Qwen judge fails the control screen. This empirical audit shows how positive relative-likelihood diagnostics can coexist with floor-level absolute model ratings in exact constrained generation. We report the construction and evaluation conditions needed to interpret these results, with frozen inputs and saved responses for reproduction. No human ratings were collected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome imposes a global constraint on a text: after spaces, punctuation, and case are removed, its letters must equal their reversal. Word boundaries need not survive this reversal. Finding dictionary words that fit the character constraint is therefore a different problem from assembling grammatical sentences with a coherent meaning. Exact palindrome generation makes this distinction directly observable: character validity can be checked exhaustively, while the resulting language still requires evaluation.

Earlier work established residual-character search for constructing long palindromes \citep{norvig2002palindrome} and graph methods for generating palindromes from n-gram corpora \citep{papadopoulos2015palindromes}. The latter already discusses the limited meaning of long constructions. For other constrained-generation tasks, \citet{bonlarron-regin-2024} combine linguistic constraints with language-model perplexity for output selection. Our question concerns the interpretation of evaluation results after construction: what does each diagnostic establish about a common set of exact outputs?

We contribute a reproducible case study that records every requested search and links each selected text to mechanical checks, shuffled controls, and model judgments. It establishes three findings on the recorded outputs: mechanically valid outputs concentrate in a recurring phrase; likelihood patterns vary across scorers and length bands; and positive shuffle-relative gains coexist with floor-level absolute model ratings under a judge that passes a small prose-control screen. The practical consequence is to report construction coverage, output variety, relative scores, and absolute ratings separately, with the controls and denominators needed to interpret each.

The study evaluates two project implementations. Its contribution is the documented relationship among these evaluation outcomes. The likelihood and rubric diagnostics measure different properties, and their coexistence supplies no human-grounded test of which metric is correct. Language judgments remain model-specific; no prior-generator superiority claim is made.

\section{Construction and evaluation}

\paragraph{Exact search.}
Let $T(x)$ retain only lowercase ASCII letters from a rendered text $x$. Exactness requires $T(x)=\mathrm{reverse}(T(x))$. Both search implementations start from empty text and use a shared frozen vocabulary and lexical gate. Outside-in search extends the outer word sequences toward their meeting point, tracking the unmatched letters owed by one side to the other; it closes when that residual is itself a palindrome. Center-out search extends away from the center and closes only on empty overhang. These native closure rules are retained, so the comparison concerns the complete implementations.

The vocabulary comes from a frozen 30K word list filtered through an English lexicon. Words of at most two letters must belong to a fixed 32-form allowlist. The shared basic gate checks supported ASCII rendering, exact palindrome equality, the target length band, lexicon membership, the short-word rule, and exclusions for listed catalogue items and templates. Repeated words are allowed. An eligible closure is rendered as space-joined words with an initial capital and final period.

Search uses English Zipf frequencies from \code{wordfreq} 3.1.1 \citep{speer-2022-wordfreq}. Its heuristic adds 0.3 per word character and subtracts 2 for each prior use of the word and 4 for an adjacent repetition. A seeded pseudorandom generator adds a fresh uniform perturbation in $[0,0.4)$ to each child's accumulated score for beam ranking. This perturbation is excluded from the score used to select the best eligible closure per normalized letter. Both implementations cap partial texts at 32 words, residual overhang at 24 letters, and text length at the target band's ceiling. Brown, GPT-2, and the prompted judges are applied afterward and do not select outputs.

\paragraph{Paired task design.}
An earlier comparison produced no basic-valid outputs in 180 runs. After toy-vocabulary checks confirmed that both implementations could reach a known target, we froze the present study on 20 fresh seeds (4000--4019), three target bands (30--49, 50--79, and 80--119 letters), two implementations, and two resource bundles: 240 runs in 60 seed-by-band blocks. The original bundle uses beam/candidate/per-parent limits of 60/200/8 and a 45-second cooperative budget; the larger bundle uses 256/800/32 and 180 seconds. Both have a 400-step ceiling. The four settings change together. This is an adaptive follow-up, and its contrast estimates the bundled change.

Coverage is the fraction of \emph{all requested runs} returning a basic-valid output. Empty and unsuccessful runs remain in the denominator. Seeds vary search trajectories, while vocabulary and lexical requirements stay fixed. Each bootstrap resample retains all 12 runs for a seed across bands, implementations, and bundles (20,000 resamples; PRNG seed 20260925). The resulting paired larger-minus-original interval describes search-seed variation, not variation across independent linguistic tasks. Length-by-strategy summaries are descriptive secondary analyses.

\paragraph{Relative language diagnostics.}
For each selected output, Brown word-order gain is its mean add-0.1 word-bigram log probability minus the mean over 32 same-word shuffles, in nats per word transition. The model is trained on 401 Brown documents, with 99 held out \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}. Scoring lowercases alphabetic tokens, maps unseen tokens to \code{<unk>}, and inserts sentence boundaries. Item-keyed shuffles preserve each item's word multiset and sentence-length sequence.

GPT-2 uses \url{openai-community/gpt2} at revision \url{607a30d783dfa663caf39e06633721c8d4cfcd7e}. Its diagnostic is candidate mean causal log probability per predicted token minus the mean for 32 archived same-word shuffles. Brown generates its own permutations, so the two scorers use different control orders. A positive difference answers whether the candidate outscores its own disordered controls under that model; it does not place the text on an absolute quality scale.

\paragraph{Prompted judgments and controls.}
DeepSeek V3.2 receives one counterbalanced candidate--shuffle pair per output with condition labels hidden (Bedrock, temperature 0, top-p 1, maximum 256 output tokens) \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekv32}. Its choices are candidate, shuffle, or tie.

Separate Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and hosted Qwen3 32B audits score grammaticality and coherent meaning on 0--3 scales \citep{qwen25,awsbedrockqwen332b}. The grammar anchors are: 0, no recoverable sentence parse; 1, isolated grammatical phrases with major sentence breaks; 2, a mostly recoverable but strained parse; 3, natural grammatical connected English. The meaning anchors are: 0, no recoverable proposition or event; 1, word associations with unclear relations; 2, a plausible but incomplete or vague interpretation; 3, clear meaning with understandable relations. The prompt instructs judges to assess the exact text without repair or rewards for formal symmetry.

Each audit has 244 presentations: 52 unique candidates, three shuffles per candidate, 12 held-out Brown prose spans and their shuffles, and 12 hidden repeats. Labels and item IDs are hidden. A joint pass requires at least 2 on both dimensions. The prespecified screen requires at least 8/12 prose controls and at most 3/12 prose shuffles to pass. It checks sensitivity to ordinary versus shuffled prose; it does not validate quality distinctions among palindromes. Both audits use temperature 0; settings and responses are archived. Hosted calls have no provider seed.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Construction yield and length.}
The original resource bundle returns 0/120 basic-valid outputs; the larger bundle returns 66/120 (55.0\%). The paired increase is 0.550, with a 95\% seed-cluster interval of [0.350, 0.750]. Each implementation succeeds on 33/60 tasks, with 11/20 successes in every length band (Table~\ref{tab:length}). Median task time is 0.014 seconds for the original bundle and 8.954 seconds for the larger one. No task reaches its deadline. The improvement concerns the full resource bundle and does not isolate beam width, branching, candidate limits, or elapsed time.

\paragraph{Mechanical validity and output variety.}
The 66 outputs contain 52 distinct strings, 26 per implementation. Every distinct string passes the normalized-letter reversal check; none reverses the word sequence. A further mechanical screen accepts 54/66 selected outputs (28 outside-in; 26 center-out). That screen requires distinct content words and excludes whole-word reversals, repeated multiword units, proper palindromic subspans, and listed templates. It is a post-selection check, rather than a rerun of search under stricter admission.

Nevertheless, 36/66 outputs contain \emph{one poem association}. The first center-out output in the frozen seed order is:
\begin{quote}\small
In as if one poem association no it a i cos same open of is an i.
\end{quote}
Its 48-letter tape is exact. The example exposes the linguistic structure left unconstrained by character equality and word membership; the repeated phrase also shows why output count and distinct wording require separate reporting.

\paragraph{Likelihood across length bands and strategies.}
Brown gains are positive for 40/66 outputs, and GPT-2 differences for 37/66. Table~\ref{tab:length} shows scorer-dependent patterns: center-out Brown gains decline from 0.584 in the shortest band to -0.129 in the longest, whereas its GPT-2 means are -0.054, 0.040, and 0.005. Outside-in mean Brown gains are positive in all bands, but the middle-band GPT-2 mean is -0.009. All 11 shortest-band outside-in outputs end at the 49-letter ceiling. Stratification thus exposes both scorer disagreement and concentration in selected closure lengths. These success-conditioned summaries do not establish an effect of length or strategy.

\paragraph{Relative preference and absolute ratings.}
DeepSeek ties all 66 candidate--shuffle pairs. In 38 responses its feature note is \emph{Both are word salad with no coherent meaning}. Table~\ref{tab:judges} reports the absolute-rating checks. Qwen2.5 assigns all candidates zero on both dimensions but passes only 1/12 prose controls; a separate post-hoc single-sentence sensitivity check passes 4/12. Both fail the control gate, making that judge's candidate scores inconclusive.

Under Qwen3, 11/12 prose controls and 0/12 prose shuffles pass jointly, satisfying the same screen. It still assigns all 52 candidates zero on both dimensions. The small screen demonstrates control sensitivity under this rubric, with a floor effect on the candidates.

\section{Discussion}

The combined results locate three different outcomes within exact constrained generation. Larger search resources recover mechanically eligible strings. Many of those strings score above their own shuffled controls under Brown or GPT-2. The same output set receives uniformly low absolute Qwen3 ratings, while that judge distinguishes the small prose-control set. Relative likelihood and absolute rubric results therefore give different views of these exact outputs.

These results identify what each favorable outcome leaves unresolved. Construction coverage counts mechanically eligible returns; recurring phrases show why it does not measure output diversity. A positive shuffle gain establishes an advantage over disordered versions of the same words, without establishing acceptable sentence quality. Qwen3's prose-control separation shows coarse sensitivity, while its candidate floor leaves no ranking among the 52 strings. Reporting these outcomes together prevents one criterion from standing in for another. Human judgments would be needed to evaluate their relationship to readers' grammaticality and meaning assessments \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}.

The search heuristic rewards word frequency and character count with local repetition penalties. It has no sentence-level grammar or meaning objective. Among outputs found under the larger bundle, Qwen3 assigns floor scores; the original bundle yields no outputs, so language quality cannot be compared across bundles. These results do not establish that the palindrome constraint itself causes poor language or that other generators share this outcome. They characterize how feasibility, length, relative likelihood, and absolute model ratings relate within this construction setup.

\section*{Reproducibility and assistance}

The supplement \url{v4-reproducibility.zip} contains frozen manifests, all requested search outcomes, selected texts and shuffles, scorer source, model revisions, prompts, and saved responses. Hash-checked joins link every score to its generated text. The length analysis and rubric totals can be regenerated offline; Brown and GPT-2 can be rescored with the pinned dependencies. Fresh hosted-model requests may differ. Brown corpus text is omitted under its terms. Generative AI assisted implementation, writing, and internal review. No participant data were collected.

\section{Limitations}

The study uses one vocabulary, three length bands, and 20 seed clusters. Seeds vary search, not independent semantic tasks. The resource follow-up is adaptive, changes four settings together, and compares implementations with different closure rules. It provides no prior-generator superiority result. Language diagnostics condition on successful search; repeated outputs and shared phrases limit their diversity. Brown and GPT-2 are fixed likelihood models, DeepSeek supplies one prompted comparison per output, and Qwen3's control set is small and partly multi-sentence. Qwen pretraining exposure to Brown is unknown; Bedrock exposes no model-weight digest or provider seed. No human ratings validate the absolute rubric or establish readability. The retrospective construction example in Appendix~\ref{sec:diana} is separate from the 240-run study.

\section{An existing construction case: the Diana frame}
\label{sec:diana}

A separate sentence-aware Brown audit records an inherited 38-letter reference and a 54-letter extension obtained through bounded noun-phrase grammar intersection over its open character frame:
\begin{quote}\small
An aide rips nine memos; some men inspire Diana.

An aide rips nine memo-hero memos. Some more home men inspire Diana.
\end{quote}
Both texts are exact letter-level palindromes. The extension preserves the inherited frame while adding noun-phrase material; it has 13 scorer tokens and two sentences, compared with nine tokens and one sentence in the reference. The audit does not establish the reference's authorship.

Brown word-order gains over 32 own-word shuffles are 0.5152 and 0.4071 nats per transition, respectively. Their prose controls, matched on scorer-token count, have gains of 0.0251 and 1.9235. Each control has one sentence. The longer candidate thus exceeds its shuffle baseline while scoring 1.5163 nats per transition below its matched prose control. This is a retrospective pair with differing sentence counts, rather than a controlled estimate of a length or construction effect. Neither text was included in the 52-candidate Qwen audits, and neither has project human ratings. Complete surfaces, score records, source hashes, and the offline analysis accompany the supplement.
