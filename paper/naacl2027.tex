\begin{abstract}
Exact letter-level palindromes can be verified mechanically; that check says nothing about the language in the text. We audit eleven selected outputs with a Brown word-bigram model and held-out prose controls. Ten of eleven matched Brown spans have higher order gain than their paired output; the nine longer constructions are lower by 1.63 nats per transition on average. As a separate check, GPT-2 assigns lower mean next-token log probability per token to ten of eleven candidates than to matched Brown spans, while every candidate scores above its own 32 same-word shuffles. DeepSeek V3.2 selected the candidate in nine of eleven counterbalanced candidate/shuffle pairs and tied in two. These are model diagnostics on retrospectively selected, related examples, not human readability results. All 108 additional Brown spans score above their own shuffles. We provide source offsets, hashes, exact model settings, outputs, and rerun commands. No human readability ratings were collected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An exact letter-level palindrome satisfies $\norm{x}=\rev(\norm{x})$, where $\norm{x}$ lowercases a rendering and removes every character except ASCII letters. This deterministic property does not characterize grammaticality, meaning, or readability.

Prior work constructs palindromes from corpus n-gram graphs or searches over unmatched character remainders \citep{papadopoulos2015palindromes,norvig2002palindrome}. Constraint programming has also been applied to generated sentences with unusual lexical and structural requirements \citep{bonlarron-regin-2024}. We study what local word order, pretrained token likelihood, and a prompted pairwise model report for selected exact outputs against held-out prose and same-word shuffles. Human evaluation requires explicit criteria and reproducible protocols; previous work documents both challenges \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}. We keep each automatic score separate from human readability.

The paper makes two bounded contributions. It gives an auditable description of the selected exact texts and their construction provenance. It then reports a Brown word-bigram comparison and two static language-model checks with held-out prose and same-word controls. These are descriptive audits of saved outputs, not a comparison of generation systems.

\section{Texts and exactness checks}

We score eleven recorded texts: an inherited 38-letter example, a 54-letter noun-phrase construction, and nine longer constructions from 498 to 752 normalized letters. The longer set includes an overhang search result, a pinned construction, a bounded relation-chain search, and selected descendants of paired edits. The descendants share construction history, and the set was selected retrospectively. Their rendered text, source artifact, construction description, and reader-status field are retained in the evidence archive.

For each rendering, the audit independently checks exactness with a raw-text outside-in scan and with reversal equality over the normalized tape. It also checks the normalized-letter count and SHA-256 digest against the selected-results manifest. These checks establish the character property for the saved renderings. They do not establish that the strings form readable English.

For context, a paired insertion around an exact parent can be written as
\begin{equation}
 p=AqC\rev(q)\rev(A), \qquad C=\rev(C),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 p'=AUqCV\rev(q)\rev(A),
\end{equation}
where $U$ and $V$ have equal length. Removing the mirrored outer string $A$ leaves $T=UqC V\rev(q)$, whose reverse is $q\rev(V)C\rev(q)\rev(U)$. Since $C$ is a palindrome, the center matches. The remaining prefix comparison is between equal-length strings $Uq$ and $q\rev(V)$; reversing that equality supplies the matching suffix. The entire tape is exact iff
\begin{equation}
 Uq=q\rev(V). \label{eq:seam}
\end{equation}
The saved 568-to-640 insertion satisfies this seam condition. Later examples include paired-span replacements and bounded clause searches, so each complete rendering is checked directly rather than relying on the insertion equation. One 752-letter rendering begins: \emph{Leon won. Wolf spots Nora. Nadia stops, so Tara rewards Nadia.} The anonymous evidence archive contains the selected-result manifest, saved tapes, lineage diffs, search replay, and exactness verifier.

\section{Local word-order diagnostic}

The scorer is an add-$\alpha$ Brown word-bigram model, with $\alpha=0.1$, trained on 401 documents from the Brown corpus. We reserve 99 documents for controls and do not train the model on those documents \citep{kucera-francis-1967,loper-bird-2002-nltk}. Tokens are lowercased matches of \code{[A-Za-z]+}; apostrophes split tokens. Candidate sentences are split after terminal punctuation, while Brown controls retain Brown sentence boundaries. The model maps unseen words to \code{<unk>} and includes one start/end boundary pair for each sentence.

For each item, \emph{order gain} is its mean log probability minus the mean score for 32 deterministic shuffles of the same word multiset. Shuffled words are repartitioned to preserve the original sentence lengths. The seed is 20260925, keyed by item identifier. A positive value means the observed order scores above its own shuffled baselines under this model; the units are nats per word transition.

Each of the eleven texts is paired with a non-overlapping span of complete held-out Brown sentences selected by nearest scorer-token count, with deterministic hash tie-breaking and no score-based selection. We also select 12 additional complete spans at each of nine target lengths (16, 32, 64, 128, 192, 256, 512, 1,024, and 2,048 tokens). The spans are non-overlapping within held-out documents. The report stores sentence offsets and text hashes, not the Brown passages. The supplement includes the pinned package versions, scorer source, saved report, and exact command to recompute every score and control.

\subsection{Static language-model checks}

We scored the same eleven candidate strings, their eleven matched Brown spans, and 32 punctuation-preserving same-word shuffles for each string with GPT-2 small \citep{radford2019language}. The checkpoint was pinned to the immutable revision cited in the model repository reference \citep{huggingfacegpt2}. For GPT-2 tokens $t_1,\ldots,t_N$, the reported value is $\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=2}^N \log p(t_i\mid t_1,\ldots,t_{i-1})$: mean causal log probability in nats per token, with the first token unscored and no truncation. Positive differences in Table~\ref{tab:static-language-verifier} mean GPT-2 assigns greater likelihood to the candidate than to the named baseline. Candidates exceed their own shuffled mean in all eleven pairs, but are below their matched Brown span in ten pairs; the paired candidate-minus-Brown mean is $-0.995$ nats per token. The 752-letter example is the one candidate slightly above its matched span ($+0.101$ nats per token).

We also asked DeepSeek V3.2 to choose the better ordinary-English passage in one counterbalanced candidate/shuffle pair per item. The prompt did not identify candidate or control roles; it requested an observable language feature and ignored palindrome status. The model selected the candidate in 9/11 pairs, tied in two, and selected the shuffle in none. We used model ID \code{deepseek.v3.2}, temperature 0, top-$p$ 1, and a 256-token maximum through Amazon Bedrock \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekv32}. The recorded 6,327 input and 217 output tokens correspond to an estimated USD 0.0043 at the published rates. A separate DeepSeek-R1 preflight used 789 input and 1,949 output tokens (estimated USD 0.0116); it was excluded because the 498-overhang request reached its output limit without a visible verdict \citep{awsbedrockdeepseekr1}. The combined Bedrock estimate is USD 0.0159 \citep{awsbedrockpricing}. Both runs and their request-level records are retained, and only V3.2 results appear in Table~\ref{tab:static-language-verifier}.

\section{Interpretation}

The Brown score records local word-order evidence relative to a same-word baseline. GPT-2 likelihood measures fit to its learned token distribution; it does not directly measure sentence meaning or reader judgments. DeepSeek V3.2's pair choices are outputs of one prompt to one model. A Brown span is a corpus control, not a reader preference label. The positive controls show that the Brown score detects ordinary corpus word order over shuffled order at the sampled lengths; none of these checks validates an automatic score as human readability.

The comparison also cannot establish a general penalty caused by palindrome constraints. The candidate texts were selected retrospectively, most share construction history, and the paired prose spans are drawn from one corpus. We make no causal, population-level, or system-ranking claim. The supplementary audit archive contains the row-level reports, scorer sources, model settings, and rerun commands. Its Brown expected-report check compares the corpus stream, held-out spans, scorer settings, and recorded scores on rerun.

\section*{Limitations}

This audit covers eleven selected texts and three automatic diagnostics: one Brown word-bigram model, GPT-2 small likelihood, and one DeepSeek V3.2 prompt. It contains no completed human ratings and no same-input comparison with a prior palindrome generator. The construction examples are finite replays, not evidence of general generation quality. None of the model scores establishes that readers find a text grammatical, coherent, or readable. A human study would need prespecified criteria, blind presentation, appropriate prose and shuffled controls, and reported agreement \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}.

\section*{Ethical considerations and assistance}
No participant data were collected. Generative AI assisted candidate construction, code, drafting, and internal review. Internal AI reviews are not human evaluation.
