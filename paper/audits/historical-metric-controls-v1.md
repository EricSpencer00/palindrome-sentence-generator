# Historical metric and control audit (v1)

Scope: saved protocols, source, and records only. No new generation, inference, or scoring. This audit separates (1) a methodological control, (2) a generation baseline, and (3) an external benchmark. Raw run IDs and records are unchanged. The current v1 edition is the public-facing audit.

## Findings

### Brown and GPT-2 same-word shuffles: retain as narrow diagnostic contrasts

The current 240-task study's 66 successful v4 outputs have frozen Brown and GPT-2 records joined to candidate texts by the saved input hashes (`runs/directional-search-resource-sensitivity-20260925/static-verifier-summary-v4-aws.json`, accompanying Brown/GPT-2 result records, and `experiments/READABILITY-LENGTH-CONSTRUCTION-RESULTS-20260926.md`). Brown's measure is candidate add-0.1 word-bigram mean log probability minus 32 deterministic own-word permutations, in nats per word transition; a held-out Brown document split, sentence boundaries, and sentence-length-preserving shuffle protocol are recorded. GPT-2 is fixed to `openai-community/gpt2` revision `607a30d…`, and its measure is mean causal log probability per predicted token minus 32 archived same-word shuffles. Brown and GPT-2 use different shuffle realizations and units. The Brown implementation (`experiments/audit_programmatic_readability.py:order_gain_by_sentence`) pools the item’s word tokens, shuffles them, then repartitions by the original per-sentence word-token counts; it preserves the exact word multiset and sentence-length sequence, but not within-sentence positions. GPT-2 uses the archived surfaces generated by `experiments/static_language_verifier_20260925.py:shuffle_surface`: it permutes alphabetic word identities into the existing word slots, preserving the word multiset, punctuation, whitespace, casing slots, and thus per-sentence word-slot counts. The word types remain the same, although GPT-2 BPE token counts can differ after reassignment.

The reported 40/66 Brown-positive and 37/66 GPT-2-positive counts are therefore valid descriptions of whether a candidate beat its own shuffled controls under these scorers. They are not an absolute language-quality scale, an estimate of human readability, a comparison against prose, or a comparison between generators. Selection is conditioned on successful generation; shared phrase repetition and only 52 distinct outputs also limit interpretation. Retain the numbers only with their metric, denominator, unit, and this boundary. Remove any free-standing “improved readability/quality” claim inferred from positive shuffle contrasts. The current manuscript mostly states this limitation correctly. Independent addition of the saved summary’s method cells gives Brown positives 15 + 25 = 40/66 and GPT-2 positives 14 + 23 = 37/66; these agree with the manuscript’s totals. The per-unit labels also agree: Brown nats per word transition and GPT-2 mean causal log probability per predicted token.

### Qwen controls: retain as a narrow calibration/sensitivity result

The frozen Qwen3 32B audit (`runs/aws-qwen3-32b-readability-audit-20260926.json`; `experiments/AWS-QWEN3-READABILITY-AUDIT-RESULTS-20260926.md`) contains 52 distinct candidates, three matched shuffles each, 12 held-out Brown prose spans and their shuffles, plus hidden repeats. Under the specified 0–3 grammar and meaning rubric, all 52 candidates score 0 on both dimensions; no candidate or its 156 shuffles passes the joint threshold. The control screen passes on 11/12 Brown spans versus 0/12 shuffles against the frozen 8/12 and 3/12 thresholds. These are chosen diagnostic-screen cutoffs, not externally validated calibration criteria. Seven Brown items are multi-sentence. The hosted model has no exposed weight digest or provider seed, and Brown pretraining exposure is unknown. Qwen candidate/control permutations use the same word-slot helper described above; punctuation, whitespace and word multiset remain fixed, including sentence word-slot counts.

This establishes only that this model/rubric distinguished these small set of ordinary Brown controls from their shuffled versions and produced a floor response on this finite candidate set. It does not validate the rubric against people, license generalization to prose or palindrome populations, or demonstrate an advantage over any generator. Retain the control result and all-zero candidate response together, explicitly label 8/12 and 3/12 as chosen diagnostic thresholds rather than validated calibration, and keep the sample-size qualifications; remove “Qwen validates readability,” any claim that the controls rank palindrome quality, and any positive-generation improvement claim. The separate Qwen2.5-3B audit failed its control gate and is inconclusive, not corroboration.

### Older mirror-cost study: frozen diagnostic, not generator evaluation

The frozen September 11 artifact is reproducible at the saved-text/arithmetic level (`experiments/mirror_cost.py`, `experiments/mirror_cost.json`, `runs/mirror-cost-2026-09-11/{results.json,audit.json,RESULTS.md}`). Its estimand is paired causal-LM surprisal difference per normalized ASCII letter for forward versus reversed versions of sampled WikiText-2 spans, with both directions segmented by the same vocabulary and each of three fixed strategies. The frozen audit verifies 36 cells, samples, segmentation, coverage, and arithmetic, but explicitly does not recompute model logits. It reports 2.14–3.34 bits/letter across GPT-2 and SmolLM2-135M cells; the revised source/report identify the frozen data and revisions.

Retain this as an operationalized, model- and segmentation-specific directional surprisal diagnostic, if needed for motivation or methods history. It is not an information-theoretic lower bound, universal “cost of making a palindrome,” generator baseline, or quality result. It samples arbitrary prose strings, not generated palindromes; reversed strings are independently resegmented; vocabulary, segmentation objective, model, and sample source define the result. Do not use its earlier shorthand “the mirror costs about 3 bits per free letter” as a general empirical law or as evidence that one generator improved. Prefer the frozen report's explicit ranges and protocol.

### Cited generators: prior-work context, not measured external comparators

The citations establish relevant prior art but the current study did not run matched external generators. Norvig's own pages document a heuristic dictionary/remainder construction and acknowledge deteriorating narrative quality; the page's outputs and runtime anecdotes are not a controlled benchmark. Papadopoulos et al. (IJCAI 2015) describe a graph method generating palindromes from n-gram corpora, with long outputs often less meaningful; the present records contain no faithful reproduction or shared-budget run. Bonlarron & Régin (IJCAI 2024) combine constraint programming and NLP properties with LM perplexity curation for RADNER sentences, a different task and constraints. None supplies a directly comparable yield, human-quality, or runtime baseline for the v4 study.

Retain these works for related-work and scope framing, accurately describing their respective methods/tasks. Remove or avoid any wording implying that v4 beats, improves on, or is state of the art against Norvig, Papadopoulos et al., or Bonlarron & Régin. The current manuscript's 0/120 vs 66/120 result is an internal resource-bundle comparison (multiple settings changed together), not an external-generator comparison. A valid external claim requires an executable faithful comparator, prespecified matched inputs/budget and outcome definition, and outcome records; no such evidence is in the cited v4 artifacts. Primary pages opened for this audit confirm these task descriptions: Norvig documents a 2016 version 3 output (90,439 letters) and letter-by-letter prefix/suffix search; his companion algorithm page separately describes versions 1–2 remainder/dictionary backtracking. Papadopoulos et al. explicitly generate from Google Ngram data through a character/word graph and describe long palindromes as often semantically weak. Bonlarron & Régin describe their Constraints First framework for RADNER sentences, combining constraint programming with linguistic and classical constraints and using LLM perplexity for curation. The URLs below resolve to these primary sources.

## Recommended claim disposition

- **Retain:** frozen within-candidate shuffle-relative Brown/GPT-2 counts, strictly as model-specific order/likelihood diagnostics; Qwen3's finite-set 11/12 versus 0/12 control separation and 0/52 candidate result as a rubric/model-specific observation; the frozen mirror-cost ranges as a protocol-bound diagnostic; cited prior methods as contextual precedent.
- **Remove:** arbitrary “improvement” claims that lack a defined comparator; any promotion of shuffle wins to absolute readability/quality; Qwen-control “validation”; universal mirror-cost claims; and superiority or state-of-the-art claims against cited generators.
- **Evidence gap:** no external generator baseline and no human ratings. The existing outputs and controls do not fill either gap.

## Cross-check against current manuscript

The reviewed manuscript’s stated Brown unit (nats per word transition), GPT-2 unit (per predicted token), and 40/66, 37/66 totals agree with the frozen records. Its Brown sentence-length-preserving shuffle description matches the implementation. The GPT-2 and Qwen implementations instead shuffle word identities within fixed surface slots; this preserves sentence word-slot counts along with punctuation/whitespace, but their scripts do not describe it as Brown’s pooled-token repartition procedure. The manuscript does not claim that the Qwen screen cutoffs are externally validated; the audit should continue calling the cutoffs prespecified/chosen diagnostic thresholds. No source-task or unit contradiction was found.
