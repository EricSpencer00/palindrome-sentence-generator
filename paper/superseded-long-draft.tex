\title{Pricing a Character-Level Constraint in Bits per Free Letter}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\stylenote

\begin{abstract}
Work on constrained generation reports how often a constraint is satisfied and
how fluent the result is. It does not report what the constraint costs. We
price one constraint in bits. Under a fixed language model and vocabulary,
forward English scores \unver{1.63} bits per letter; the same letters reversed
and re-segmented optimally into that vocabulary score \unver{4.92}. A
character-level palindrome constraint therefore costs \unver{3.30} bits per
free letter. Each letter is placed twice and both placements must be English,
so the set of sequences that read in both directions thins by roughly an order
of magnitude per free letter, which predicts where readable output stops. We
test the prediction with seven construction routes: beam search with
language-model scoring, exhaustive enumeration of the short regime, mining
attested corpus $n$-grams, closed-form reversible-word chains, model authoring,
authoring one half and segmenting the other, and a sharded vocabulary walk.
They stop in the same region. Of 34{,}688 attested four-grams, none has a
mirror whose two halves both read, and a strict rubric calibrated to accept
real prose and reject word salad passes 0 of 170 units drawn from five
independently selected populations. Four automatic proxies were sound as
filters and failed as rankers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long palindromes do not read. Norvig's record-length palindrome is a list of
nouns its author describes as nonsense, half the canonical short palindromes
run between 12 and 17 letters, and \citet{papadopoulos2015} note in passing
that long palindromes tend to be less meaningful than short ones. The
observation is old and repeated. It has not been attached to a number, so it
functions as a fact about a genre rather than as a quantity that predicts
anything or transfers to another constraint.

We measure it. A palindrome of $2k$ letters has $k$ free letters: the second
half is the first half reversed, so each letter is chosen once, placed twice,
and the two placements segment into unrelated words. That yields an estimator.
Score English forward under a language model and a vocabulary; reverse the same
letters, re-segment them optimally into the same vocabulary, and score the
result. The difference is what the constraint charges per free letter. Under
GPT-2 small \citep{radford2019}, forward English scores \unver{1.63} bits per
letter and the reversed, re-segmented text scores \unver{4.92}, giving
\unver{3.30} bits per free letter, stable across span lengths and segmentation
strategies. Read as a rate, that is a factor of about ten per free letter: the
set of letter sequences that read as English in both directions thins
geometrically in length.

The palindrome is a more useful instrument than its reputation suggests,
because it is cheap to satisfy and expensive to satisfy well. It is cheap to
satisfy because the constraint has a sufficient statistic that makes it
checkable incrementally. In a two-sided construction, one half owes the other a
run of letters, the \emph{overhang}. A word added on the left must match the
overhang forwards; a word added on the right must match it reversed; the text
closes when the overhang is itself a palindrome. The search is Norvig's
\citeyearpar{norvig2002}, building on Hoey's 1984 program, and our contribution
sits above it rather than in it. What the search buys is that validity comes
from the search and not from the model. Every candidate returned in every
configuration we ran was a valid palindrome: 12 of 12 and 24 of 24 at the
matched budgets used for language-model ablations, and 200 of 200 at each of
five settings of the beam's exploration parameter. A model in this pipeline
cannot make the output invalid and cannot make it valid. It can only make the
output more or less readable. Benchmarks in which the model enforces the
constraint cannot separate those two axes, because a satisfaction gain and a
fluency gain arrive together.

We tested the prediction from seven directions, chosen so that they would fail
for different reasons if they failed. Mining 272k attested bigrams yields 3{,}894
mirror-pairs, of which 131 have both halves attested; at three-word halves, 27;
at four-word halves, where prose would begin, 0 from 34{,}688 attested
four-grams. Of 148 authored sentences, 12 have any spellable mirror and none
whose mirror reads. In the 40--60 letter band, 880k closures produced none
within three unattested joins of reading. Under a rubric calibrated to accept
real prose 5/5 and reject word salad 0/5, five independently selected
populations drawn from an exhaustive walk of 2.55M short palindromes passed 0
of 170. The system does not produce coherent novel palindromic prose. Its
readable output is assembled from catalogued palindromes, and its fully
generated mode returns two- and three-word fragments.

Two further results come from the same measurements. Ranking is where the
automatic signals break: four proxies were each checked against blinded
judgment with real-prose and word-salad controls, and each was usable as a
filter and unusable as a ranker, the language-model proxy on three separate
occasions. The proxies are also gameable in ways that a check against a
stronger model does not catch. Optimising a per-letter-normalised
language-model score gained 0.298 while the same texts scored 0.673 worse per
token, losing 24 of 24 paired seeds, because the policy had found longer words
rather than better text.

Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep0em
\item A price for a formal textual constraint, in bits per free letter, with an
estimator that reimplements in a page of code, and its stability across span
lengths and segmentation strategies (\S\ref{sec:cost}).
\item A ceiling derived from that price and tested from seven construction
routes, with the negative results reported in full (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}).
\item A separation between the filter role and the ranker role of proxy
metrics, with blinded judgment on both sides and two demonstrated gaming modes
(\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\item Artifacts: mined mirror-pairs with attestation flags, judged
self-palindromic centres, catalogued palindromes stored with their spelling,
generated novel pairs, and a nine-criterion conjunctive task definition whose
test suite fails by design (Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}).
\end{enumerate}

The estimator does not depend on anything specific to mirrors, which is the
reason to care about it without caring about palindromes. Any formal constraint
that restricts which strings are admissible has a price in the same units,
obtained the same way: score the unconstrained text, score the constrained text
under the same model and vocabulary, take the difference per unit of free
choice. A lipogram removes letters from the alphabet and its price is the
entropy of what was removed. A rhyme scheme constrains line-final words and its
price is per rhymed position. Meter, acrostics, and output-format grammars such
as a JSON schema restrict the continuation set at every step and admit the same
arithmetic. Format restrictions are already known to degrade model reasoning
\citep{tam2024}; what has been missing is a way to say by how much, in units
that compare across constraints. The palindrome is the case where the
arithmetic can be validated, because the constraint is severe enough that the
ceiling is reachable with a laptop and a dictionary.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Palindrome generation.}
The closest prior work is \citet{papadopoulos2015}, which gives a graph
structure that enumerates \emph{all} palindromes obtainable from an $n$-gram
corpus in time linear in the size of the structure, handles the simultaneous
character-level and word-level coupling that makes the constraint awkward to
express, and steers output semantically by biasing word probabilities from an
auxiliary corpus. Their contribution is completeness: given a corpus, the
reachable set is characterised and can be walked. We take that as given and ask
a question they do not address. Their completeness result strengthens our
negative results rather than weakening them. If the reachable set can be
enumerated in full, a search that finds nothing readable is reporting a
property of the space rather than a failure of the search, and our exhaustive
walk of 2.55M short palindromes with 0 of 170 strict passes is the empirical
form of that argument. Their semantic steering is a \emph{ranking} method over
the enumerated set, and ranking is where our proxy audit finds automatic
signals fail on this material. The two results are complementary: theirs says
what is reachable, ours says what reaching it costs. The algorithmic ancestor
of our search is \citet{norvig2002}, building on Hoey's 1984 program;
\citet{cppgm2020} generate Chinese palindrome poetry under a different
constraint at a different level.

\paragraph{Constrained decoding.}
Lexically constrained decoding enforces membership constraints during search
\citep{hokamp2017,post2018}, and NeuroLogic \citep{lu2021} extends this to
predicate logic over lexical constraints, with lookahead heuristics added in
NeuroLogic A*esque \citep{lu2022}. The lookahead framing is the nearest
analogue to our overhang trie. COLD \citep{qin2022} treats the problem as
energy-based sampling. Grammar- and automaton-constrained decoding
\citep{willard2023,beurerkellner2024} enforces formal languages, and
grammar-aligned decoding \citep{park2024} shows that the naive form distorts
the model distribution rather than conditioning on it. Sequential Monte Carlo
steering \citep{lew2023,loula2025} gives the principled route to conditioning
on a hard constraint. We discuss in \S\ref{sec:formalisation} why the overhang
is the sufficient statistic that would make an SMC formulation of this
constraint tractable. \citet{garbacea2022} survey why constrained neural
generation is hard. Across this literature the reported quantities are
satisfaction rate and fluency; the price of the constraint is not among them.

\paragraph{Entropy of English.}
Our estimator descends from \citet{shannon1951}, who bounded the entropy of
printed English at roughly 1.3 bits per character using human prediction, and
from the gambling estimate of \citet{coverking1978}. The dependence of any
model-derived figure on the model is discussed in \S\ref{sec:limitations}.
Linguistic steganography measures the adjacent quantity: how many bits of
arbitrary payload text can absorb per word before it stops reading
\citep{chang2014}. The palindrome cost is the same currency pointed the other
way.

\paragraph{Directionality.}
Autoregressive models trained left to right fail to recall relations in reverse
\citep{berglund2023}, an effect traced to which tokens are predicted rather
than to the architecture \citep{kitouni2024}. Reverse training
\citep{golovneva2024} and reverse language models \citep{ledom2025} address it
directly. Infilling objectives \citep{bavarian2022,shen2020} address the
related problem of generating text conditioned on both sides. A palindrome is a
two-sided infilling problem in which the two sides constrain each other through
a channel that is not the text.

\paragraph{Character-level competence.}
Subword tokenization impedes character-level manipulation \citep{edman2024},
which is why prompting a model directly is not a substitute for search here,
and why the separation between the search's guarantee and the model's
contribution is available in this task at all.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Automatic metrics correlate weakly with human judgment on creative text
\citep{lau2018,potash2016}. Reward models and model judges exhibit length bias
\citep{dubois2024,zheng2023}, which is the general form of the normalisation
result in \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\section{Problem and formalisation}
\label{sec:formalisation}

Let $\mathrm{norm}(t)$ strip a text to its lowercase letters. A text $t$ is a
palindrome iff $\mathrm{norm}(t)$ equals its own reverse. Case, spacing and
punctuation are therefore free: they carry no constraint and can be assigned
after the letters are fixed, which is what makes readable rendering possible at
all.

Build a text from both ends inward. At any point the state is a left prefix
$L$ and a right suffix $R$, and the constraint on the unwritten middle is
captured entirely by the \emph{overhang}: the run of letters one side owes the
other, $\mathrm{norm}(L)$ and $\mathrm{norm}(R)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ agreeing on
their common length with one of them extending past the other by that run. A
word appended on the left must match the overhang forwards; a word prepended on
the right must match it reversed. The construction closes when the overhang is
itself a palindrome, which becomes the centre.

The overhang is a sufficient statistic for the constraint: two states with the
same overhang admit exactly the same set of completions, regardless of how they
were reached. This is what makes the constraint checkable incrementally, and it
is the property an SMC formulation would need, since the incremental
feasibility check is what a particle filter requires to avoid degenerate
weights. It is also why validity is free here while quality is not.

\paragraph{What ``reads'' means.}
We use a nine-criterion conjunctive definition, given in full in
Appendix~\ref{app:criteria}: minimum length, whole-text palindromicity, at most
one self-palindromic sentence, no repeated sentence, disjoint halves,
grammaticality, a nameable subject, coherence judged against both real-prose
and disparate-real-sentence controls, and novelty against a catalogue of 160
known palindromes. The criteria are conjunctive rather than a score to average.
Failing one is failing. We adopted this form after four separate constructions
in this project satisfied a majority of the criteria while abandoning the
constraint that made the task hard.

\section{Cost of the constraint}
\label{sec:cost}

\paragraph{Estimator.}
Fix a language model $M$ and a frequency-ranked vocabulary $V$. For a span of
English text $s$, let $H_{\rightarrow}(s)$ be the model's cross-entropy in bits
per letter over $s$. Let $\rho(s)$ be $\mathrm{norm}(s)$ reversed and
re-segmented into $V$ by the segmentation that minimises cross-entropy, and let
$H_{\leftarrow}(s) = H_{\rightarrow}(\rho(s))$. The price of the mirror is

\[ \Delta = H_{\leftarrow}(s) - H_{\rightarrow}(s) \]

in bits per free letter, since a palindrome fixes each free letter once and
pays for it in both readings.

\paragraph{Result.}
Under GPT-2 small over a frequency-ranked vocabulary of \tk{size},
$H_{\rightarrow} = \unver{1.63}$ and $H_{\leftarrow} = \unver{4.92}$, giving
$\Delta = \unver{3.30}$ bits per free letter, \unver{stable across span lengths
and segmentation strategies}. Model scale replication is reported in
\tk{Table~2}.

\paragraph{Consequence.}
A cost of $\Delta$ bits per free letter means the admissible set contracts by a
factor of $2^{\Delta} \approx \unver{9.8}$ for each free letter added. The
prediction is that coherent output is available at short lengths and
unavailable beyond a threshold that this rate places within reach of
enumeration, which is what \S\ref{sec:ceiling} tests.

\section{Systems}
\label{sec:systems}

Two systems were built. Neither is the contribution; they are the instruments
that produce the candidates \S\ref{sec:ceiling} evaluates.

\paragraph{Search with model scoring.}
Beam search over the overhang trie, scored by word frequency, with GPT-2
applied either to the finished candidates (rerank) or inside the beam (in-loop).
At matched budgets over 12 seeds at a 200-letter minimum and beam 60, rerank
reaches a best score of $-2.382$ and a mean of $-2.455$ in 5.5s; in-loop
reaches $-2.297$ and $-2.405$ in 170s, about $31\times$ the cost. Both arms
return 12 valid palindromes from 12 seeds. Validity is identical across the two arms, so the
model moves readability and nothing else. Which arm scores higher is
secondary.

Both figures are per letter, which \S\ref{sec:eval} shows is a normalisation
that word length can move. The two arms run the same scorer over the same
vocabulary and differ only in when the model is applied, so word length does not
separate them, but the comparison has not been repeated per token.

\paragraph{Compositional assembly.}
Units that satisfy the constraint internally nest like brackets:
$L_1 L_2 \ldots L_k\, C\, R_k \ldots R_2 R_1$ is a palindrome by construction
when each $L_i$/$R_i$ pair spells the other backwards and $C$ is
self-palindromic. Length stops being the binding problem and unit selection
becomes it, and the selection subproblem carries no letter constraint at all.

Three structures can pay a mirror across a paragraph. Free-running search
places one mirror across the whole text; it repeats nothing and has no
independent units, and it is what the 40--60 letter measurements in
\S\ref{sec:ceiling} bound. Self-palindromic units force sequence repetition:
if every unit is itself a palindrome, the concatenation reverses into those
same units in the opposite order, so the whole is palindromic only when unit
$k$ equals unit $n{+}1{-}k$. That is forced by the algebra rather than chosen
for effect. Mirror-pairs, where the unit is two halves that spell each other
backwards placed at mirrored positions, is the only structure that repeats
nothing, because what returns at position $n{+}1{-}k$ is the other half.

\section{Construction routes and where they stop}
\label{sec:ceiling}

Seven routes, chosen so that they would fail for different reasons.
Table~\ref{tab:routes} collects them.

\paragraph{Exhaustive enumeration.}
At 24 letters the space is walkable, so the model stops steering and starts
choosing from all of it. Enumeration is exhaustive only at small vocabularies:
with 14k units the tree branches roughly 14k wide at every closure, so a
time-budgeted walk returns a deep prefix of one corner. We use canon recall,
the fraction of catalogued palindromes a walk rediscovers, as the acceptance
test: 10 of 18 at 83 canon-seeded words and depth 4, and 0 of 27 at 14k.

\paragraph{Mining attested corpus $n$-grams.}
272k attested bigrams yield 3{,}894 mirror-pairs, 131 with both halves attested.
At three-word halves the counts are 762 and 27. At four-word halves, where
prose would begin, they are 106 and \textbf{0}, from 34{,}688 attested
four-grams. Thematic selection over the 26 usable halves is also unavailable:
of every content-word pair across them, 2 co-occur anywhere in 3{,}932
sentences.

\paragraph{Free-running search at prose length.}
The 40--60 letter band yields 880k closures, 112{,}523 of the first 300{,}000
using only dictionary words, and none within three unattested joins of reading.
A paragraph needs roughly 500 letters.

\paragraph{Authoring one half.}
The division of labour that puts the writing where writing is good: a model
writes the left half, segmentation recovers the right from its reversed
letters. Of 148 authored sentences of three to six words, 12 have any spellable
mirror and 0 have a mirror that reads. The opening word of one half is the
closing word of the other, so a half written to start well ends the other half
badly, and selecting better sentences cannot fix a constraint that runs the
other way.

\paragraph{Reversible words in closed form.}
392 reversible words yield 13{,}924 candidate pairs, 5 with both halves
attested, 2 usable. Walked exhaustively for word-aligned chains, 57 joins are
attested on both sides of the mirror and produce 4 novel chains, all of one
template.

\paragraph{Best-of-$N$ over the search's output.}
Best-of-$N$ over 2000 palindromes scored by Qwen2.5-0.5B \citep{qwen2024}
improves from $-6.978$ at $N{=}24$ to $-6.810$ at $N{=}2000$, and the last
thousand samples buy 0.007 nats per token. Best-of-$N$ upper-bounds any
reranker on a fixed pool, so no better judge helps here. The bound is over one
proposal distribution, and that distribution turned out to be narrow: of the
2000 candidates, 1975 opened with the same five words, 1983 closed with the
same five, and the sample used 1{,}317 of 30{,}000 available words.

\paragraph{Beam exploration.}
Raising the beam's exploration parameter across five settings takes distinct
openings from 3 to 200 and vocabulary in use from 708 to 2{,}854 words, at no
cost in closure (200 of 200 at every setting). Mean readability falls
monotonically by 0.49 nats per token across the range. Unanchored from word
frequency the search reaches for rare proper nouns and the text gets worse.

\paragraph{Attestation filters against the catalogue.}
Constraining the walk so that every join inside a half is one the bigram counts
record makes output look better. Run against the 29 catalogued mirror-pairs the
filter is meant to find more of, \texttt{count\_2w} covers every join in 27\% of
their halves, Brown's million words 29\%, and GPT-2's top-400 followers 27\%. A
pair needs both halves, so the constraint discards roughly 93\% of the target.
``Evil rats on / no star live'' has no attested join anywhere. The constraint
remains available and is not the default.

\section{Construction direction}
\label{sec:direction}

Both growth directions build one half by appending words and the other by
prepending them. A fluency gap between the halves isolates the cost of building
text backwards, since the halves share a letter sequence.

Measured per letter, the appended half scores $+0.443$ above the prepended one,
reproducing an earlier figure of $+0.368$. Measured per token on the same 24
seeds at the same budget, the gap is $-0.824$: the prepended half scores better.
The halves segment the same letters into different words and the prepended half
carries shorter ones, so the per-letter gap is a fact about word length and not
about readability. We report both and draw no directional conclusion from
either.

What survives is the intervention. A model fine-tuned on reversed token order,
used as a scoring term inside the search, beats its matched forward control at
every weight swept ($+0.177$, $+0.080$, $+0.157$ per token) and beats the plain
frequency baseline by $+0.423$ per token at the best weight, with 24 of 24
closure at every setting. The best arm per token is the worst arm per letter,
so the per-letter normalisation would have discarded the intervention. Two
limits: the gain grows monotonically across the swept range, so the best weight
tested is the edge of the sweep rather than an optimum, and at the full 30k
vocabulary single-token scoring is exact for 58.6\% of words, so the backward
term is approximate for two words in five.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Candidates are judged in blinded batches with the key withheld until after
scoring, and every batch carries two calibration controls: a real-prose item
and a word-salad item built by shuffling. A batch in which the controls are not
separated is discarded. The strict rubric asks whether a text reads as
intentional \emph{and} says something, and is calibrated to accept real prose
5/5 and reject salad 0/5. \tk{Annotator count, items per condition, and
agreement statistic: see \S\ref{sec:limitations}.}

\paragraph{Filter role and ranker role.}
Table~\ref{tab:proxies} gives four proxies against blinded judgment. Each is
usable for exclusion. None ranks finished text.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
proxy & as filter & as ranker \\
\midrule
GPT-2 score & sound & failed, 3 times \\
attested-join rate & 79\% vs 38\% & not used \\
thematic cohesion & finds the theme & failed \\
word frequency & no signal & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Proxy metrics against blinded judgment with real-prose and
word-salad controls.}
\label{tab:proxies}
\end{table}

Four surface proxies were separately killed against labelled verdicts: GPT-2
weaker-half score (AUC 0.659), full bigram-join attestation, which rejects the
best unit found, rule-based vocabulary filtering (0 of 20), and edge-join
attestation, which gave no separation.

\paragraph{Two gaming modes.}
A policy optimising a per-letter-normalised model score gained $0.298$ while
the same texts scored $0.673$ worse per token, losing 24 of 24 paired seeds.
The policy had found longer words. Longer words cost fewer tokens per letter,
so a search that prefers them raises the metric without writing anything
better, and the anchor model missed it because the anchor used the same
normalisation as the target.

Second, a long-range coherence gain, which asks whether a text's opening
predicts its ending, can be won roughly eight times over by constructions that
write nothing: repeating the head as the tail verbatim, among others. This
check was run before any optimiser was pointed at the metric.

\paragraph{Judge instability.}
Borderline items flip on repeat judging at roughly even odds, and three
findings in this project were retracted after failing to reproduce under
re-judging. Single-verdict results on borderline material should not be
treated as measurements.

\section{Direct prompting baseline}
\label{sec:llm}

\tk{Protocol specified, results pending.} Frontier models are prompted for
palindromes at target lengths of 30, 60, 120 and 200 letters, across
\tk{$k$} prompt formulations with \tk{$n$} samples per cell. Validity is scored
by the same normaliser used throughout, and novelty against the 160-entry
catalogue, because a model authoring round in this project returned 20 claimed
novel palindromes of which all 20 were catalogued classics. Whatever passes
validity is judged under the protocol in \S\ref{sec:eval}. The
character-level literature \citep{edman2024} predicts failure at validity,
which if confirmed is the cleanest available demonstration that the search and
not the model is what makes the constraint hold.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Pricing other constraints.}
The estimator needs three things: a model, a vocabulary, and a well-defined
notion of free choice. A lipogram removes letters from the alphabet, and the
free unit is the letter position; a rhyme scheme constrains line-final words,
and the free unit is the rhymed position; a syllable-count meter constrains
positions per line; an output-format grammar restricts the continuation set at
every step, and the free unit is the token. In each case, score unconstrained
text, score constrained text under the same model and vocabulary, and divide by
free units. The resulting numbers are comparable across constraints in a way
that satisfaction rates are not.

\paragraph{What a solution would require.}
The cost bounds unit length, not palindrome length. Whole self-palindromic
sentences pay the same price and do carry subjects, which is why compositional
assembly reaches lengths that free-running search does not. Any route to
coherent novel palindromic prose has to produce units long enough to carry a
subject and not already in the record. Four-word halves are where prose would
start, and 34{,}688 attested four-grams produced none.

\paragraph{Reporting proxy roles.}
Papers that use an automatic signal should state whether it filters or ranks.
The validation burden differs: a filter needs a false-rejection rate against
accepted items, a ranker needs correlation with judgment over the range where
it will be applied. In this project every proxy that passed the first test
failed the second, and the failures were invisible until judgment was
collected.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The cost estimate rests on one small model. Replication across scales and
families is \tk{reported in Table~2}; until it is in, the number should be read
as model-dependent, and the estimator, not the value, is the transferable part.
The vocabulary size used for the measurement is \tk{unrecorded}, and the
stability claim across span lengths and segmentation strategies is asserted in
our notes and \unver{not tabulated}.

The judging protocol is blinded and control-calibrated, and it is
\tk{single-judge}: annotator count, items per condition, agreement statistic
and verbatim instructions are not established for the results reported here.
Every judgment-dependent claim in \S\ref{sec:ceiling} and \S\ref{sec:eval}
inherits that limit.

Results are English-only, and the segmentation step assumes a writing system
with a letter inventory small enough to enumerate.

The system does not produce coherent novel palindromic prose. Output that reads
is assembled from catalogued palindromes written by other people; the assembly,
ordering and selection are ours and the sentences are not. The fully generated
mode produces two- and three-word fragments at the required length. Provenance
is tracked per unit and enforced by a novelty check against 160 catalogued
entries.

Two rows in our own records did not survive re-measurement and are reported
here as retracted rather than removed: the directional claim in
\S\ref{sec:direction}, and three earlier findings that failed to reproduce
under re-judging.

\section{Conclusion}

A character-level palindrome constraint costs \unver{3.30} bits per free letter
under a fixed model and vocabulary, which predicts that the coherent feasible
set thins by roughly an order of magnitude per free letter. Seven construction
routes stop in the region that rate predicts, and the sharpest of them finds no
readable mirror among 34{,}688 attested four-grams. Automatic proxies for
readability are usable to exclude candidates and unusable to rank them. The
estimator applies to any constraint that restricts admissible strings, and
lipograms and rhyme schemes are the two cases where it should be checked next.
