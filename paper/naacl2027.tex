\begin{abstract}
We examine one way to extend exact letter-level palindromes: edit two paired locations while keeping the rest of the text. For equal-length insertions, exactness reduces to a local equation across the retained bridge. A saved lineage grows from 568 to 752 letters through one insertion and three replacements of paired spans; a separate bounded search yields an exact 672-letter text. Rechecks and ablations confirm the character constraints, but not the quality of the prose. Most wording was authored, and no reader study or language-quality comparison was conducted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A letter-level palindrome has the same sequence of ASCII letters in both directions, ignoring case and all other characters. Making one longer means finding wording that fits the character pattern while still reading as prose. Earlier work searches from unmatched remainders or builds palindromes from n-gram corpora \citep{norvig2002palindrome,papadopoulos2015palindromes}; related text-generation work handles lexical and structural constraints during decoding \citep{hokamp2017grid,bonlarron-regin-2024}.

We start with an exact palindrome and change text at paired locations. The report follows a saved lineage from 568 to 752 letters and reproduces a bounded search that finds a 672-letter text. The local condition below describes the first insertion; later replacements are checked on the complete text.

\section{Paired insertion condition}

For rendered text $x$, $\norm{x}$ lowercases and keeps only ASCII letters. We call $x$ a palindrome when $\norm{x}=\rev(\norm{x})$. Write an exact parent as
\begin{equation}
 p=AqC\rev(q)\rev(A),\qquad C=\rev(C),
\end{equation}
Here $q$ is the retained bridge between the edit positions and $C$ is the center. Insert $U$ after $A$ and $V$ after $C$:
\begin{equation}
 p'=AUqCV\rev(q)\rev(A).
\end{equation}
When $|U|=|V|$, the new text is a palindrome exactly when
\begin{equation}
 Uq=q\rev(V). \label{eq:seam}
\end{equation}
Once the mirrored outer $A$ is set aside, the two prefixes beside $C$ are $Uq$ and $q\rev(V)$. If they match, reversing that equality matches the suffix too, and the center already reads the same in both directions. This condition applies to the first insertion in the 568-to-640 step; the later replacements are checked directly against the full palindrome.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Saved lineage.} The branch grows from 568 to 752 letters in four edits: one paired insertion, followed by three replacements of paired spans. Equation~\ref{eq:seam} describes the insertion only; each later output is checked against the complete palindrome condition. Table~\ref{tab:lineage} shows the five saved stages. Each stage inherits the previous text, so these are successive edits, not independent trials. The repetition statistics describe repeated wording; they do not tell us whether the passage reads well.

The 752-letter endpoint passes both exactness checks (normalized SHA-256 begins \code{109c758e} and ends \code{f5106f222de3c}). From 686 to 752 letters, the number of distinct word types falls from 56 to 48, while the repeated-trigram rate rises from 4.8\% to 6.6\%. The passage still contains rough clauses and a proper palindromic span. Its full text and replay materials are in the companion evidence archive.

\paragraph{Other checks.} A bounded search inserts four clauses at two seams of the 568-letter parent. It returns one exact 672-letter result after examining 9,273 states, rejecting 35 attempts, and accepting one path. The inserted clauses are: \emph{Nora stops Leon. Leon stops Nora. Nora stops Noel. Noel spots Nora. Aron stops Leon. Leon spots Aron. Aron spots Noel. Noel spots Aron.} The clauses are formulaic. In an ablation, the complete 568-to-630 paired edit is exact, while either one-sided insertion fails. The equation check found no mismatch in 54,145 equal-length cases or 91 context cases.

\paragraph{Scope.} These results come from selected saved outputs, and several share a parent. We did not ask readers to rate them or compare their language quality with another system. Studies of generated text use explicit criteria and repeatable protocols \citep{howcroft-etal-2020-twenty,khashabi-etal-2022-genie}. Our checks measure symmetry and replay; they do not measure fluency, coherence, or reader understanding.
