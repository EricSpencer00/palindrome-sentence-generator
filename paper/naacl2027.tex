\begin{abstract}
We study the constructive generation of long English palindromes under a
strict letter-level definition.  A candidate must be an exact palindrome,
originate from an auditable construction rather than borrowed catalogue text,
avoid repeated or self-palindromic units, and survive a blinded human
readability study.  Our workbench keeps character obligations live while it
builds ordinary scenes: typed grammar and semantic slots propose the next
words, and targeted repairs are selected from the current mirrored-letter
debt.  Ten deliberately orthogonal construction lanes share an independent
two-pointer and hash verifier, provenance records, and a novelty registry.
The current development snapshot contains 4,845 candidates from 226 route
summaries; 79 are exact, but none passes the mechanical admission gate and no row has
yet been promoted by readers.  We report the rendered candidates and the
failed gates explicitly, then use each failure to define the next repair
operator.  The contribution is therefore a reproducible construction and
evaluation protocol aimed at a genuinely readable long output, not a speed
benchmark or a proxy-only result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task and acceptance gate}
Let $x$ be a rendered English passage.  We normalize by lowercasing and
retaining ASCII letters only,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{x}=\operatorname{letters}(\operatorname{lower}(x)),
\end{equation}
and require $\norm{x}=\rev(\norm{x})$.  Punctuation and spacing are displayed
for readers but cannot change the equation.  The target is an intact,
multi-clause passage of at least 100 normalized letters; the familiar
38-letter seed is used only as a development benchmark.

The hard gate has four independent parts.  First, a standard-library
two-pointer check and a separately implemented forward/reverse hash check
must agree.  Second, the provenance ledger must show that the text was
constructed by the current method, with no catalogue import, scaffold
relexicalization, repeated multiword unit, word-order reflection, or
self-palindromic word/span.  Third, the rendering must be ordinary prose with
distinct lexical choices, not a list or fragment.  Finally, blinded readers
must judge it readable and coherent.  Programmatic language scores can rank
or diagnose candidates; they never certify the last condition.

\section{A working construction loop}
The constructor maintains a state
$(L,R,d,\sigma_L,\sigma_R,\rho)$, where $L$ and $R$ are the committed left
and right tapes, $d$ is the vector of mirrored character debt, $\sigma$ stores
grammar and agreement state, and $\rho$ is the semantic scene graph.  At each
step it (i) chooses a typed scene slot or boundary, (ii) proposes a short
ordinary word or inflection compatible with $\sigma$ and $\rho$, (iii) updates
the opposing character obligation immediately, and (iv) retains the best
frontier by length and unresolved debt.  A repair may replace a complete
semantic slot, resegment an exact tape, change a dependency seam, or alter an
inflection/clitic boundary.  A run is not counted as progress when it merely
resamples the same lane or duplicates a prior signature.

This is a character-constrained generation problem in the spirit of prior
palindrome enumeration and lexically constrained decoding
\citep{papadopoulos2015palindromes,hokamp2017grid,lu2022astar}, but the
character obligation is coupled to a live scene and grammar rather than
applied after a sentence has been written.

The ten lanes are intentionally different views of this same loop:
character-level LM decoding; exact-tape grammatical resegmentation;
dependency-tree seam CSP; agreement-carrying morphology; CFG/Earley character
intersection; a human-authored scene lattice with live equations; semantic
valency and attachment; inflectional and clitic boundary search; a flat,
compositional grammar without nested palindrome spans; and exact-candidate
repair by semantic slot substitution.  Each lane writes its rendered text,
normalized tape, independent checks, source hashes, novelty result, and a
concrete next repair to a JSON artifact.

The implementation also exposes an explicit totality witness for arbitrary
requested lengths.  This witness is useful for testing the state machine but
is tagged as non-reader output and cannot satisfy the admission gate.  Thus
termination of the software is never confused with success of the linguistic
task.

\section{Independent verification and provenance}
For every row, the verifier recomputes normalization, length, first mismatch,
two-pointer equality, and a forward/reverse digest without importing the
generator's booleans.  The mechanical admission vector records ASCII support,
lexical form, distinctness, catalogue-family exclusion, repeated-unit and
self-palindrome exclusion, and non-word-order symmetry.  The registry assigns
one signature to each run and rejects duplicate families before aggregation.
On the authoritative snapshot the registry contains 327 unique artifacts (31
preflight exclusions); the aggregate contains 4,845 rows, 79 exact rows, and
zero mechanically admitted rows across 226 route summaries.  Every exact row
therefore has a visible reason for rejection or promotion status rather than
being silently discarded.  The aggregate report is registered separately as
an audit artifact, never as a construction family.

\section{Reader-facing evaluation}
No candidate is called readable by the automatic diagnostics.  The release
package is gated until a mechanically admitted survivor exists.  The planned
study uses intact prose and controls within five normalized letters,
randomized blinded order, opaque item identifiers with a separate key, and a
reproducible rater package.  The builder requires structured provenance with
immutable generator/source hashes and explicit false flags for copied or
borrowed text and shortcut symmetries; it also regenerates controls until their
normalized tapes are changed and non-palindromic.  Readers rate Englishness,
coherence, and grammaticality
independently; two blinded coders adjudicate free-form paraphrases.  The
analysis is preregistered before unblinding, with the candidate admitted only
if it beats the controls on the prespecified reader criteria; agreement will
be reported with a preregistered reliability statistic such as Krippendorff's
alpha \citep{krippendorff2011computing}.  The current
manuscript reports no human responses and makes no readability claim.

\section{Limitations and next construction}
The present snapshot has not yet produced a $\geq100$-letter candidate that
passes all gates.  That is a development status, not the intended contribution.
The next run will change the construction state rather than repeat a sweep:
the seam lane will use typed boundary resegmentation and then a fresh
non-palindromic subject/verb/object frame while preserving agreement; the scene
lanes will solve an object/attachment slot against the live tape; and the LM
lane will propose new clause boundaries on held-out vocabulary.
Each resulting surface will be rendered and audited before a reader-facing
test.  Once a survivor appears, the reader package and paper tables will be
regenerated from its immutable provenance record.
